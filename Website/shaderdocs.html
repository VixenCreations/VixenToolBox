VixenWear Latex Ultra Inspector

A complete control-by-control reference for the VixenWear Latex Ultra inspector. The material inspector is driven by the VixForge Editor, and this page is laid out the same way: one section per inspector section, in the order the inspector lists them.

Built-in Render Pipeline / VRChat only - it does not compile under HDRP or URP. Every world-lighting integration (Light Volumes, LTCGI, VRSL) is fail-safe: it strips or no-ops cleanly in worlds that lack the system.

BASE

Section 1 of 12

Render mode, albedo, brightness floor, and UV animation.

Base

  • Rendering Mode - Selects the blend preset: Opaque, Cutout, Fade, or Transparent. This single dropdown drives blend state, depth writing, render queue, and the fallback tags. Fade uses straight alpha; Transparent uses premultiplied alpha so specular survives at low opacity (the correct mode for glass and wet-look latex); Cutout uses a hard alpha-clip threshold.
  • CutOff - The alpha clip threshold, shown only in Cutout mode.
  • Use Mask Texture + Cutout Mask + Mask Channel - Cutout mode normally reads the Albedo's alpha. If your texture has no alpha, turn this on and give it a black-and-white mask instead: white keeps the pixel, black cuts it away. Shown only in Cutout mode.
  • Color - The main colour tint multiplied into the albedo. In Fade and Transparent its alpha sets the opacity, so a material can go see-through with no texture at all.
  • Albedo - The main texture slot. RGB is the albedo; its Alpha channel is the Cutout threshold in Cutout mode and multiplies the Color's opacity in Fade / Transparent. Leave it empty to set opacity from the Color alone.
  • Minimum Brightness - A floor on the final lit result so the surface never crushes to pure black in unlit or very dark worlds.
  • Use Alpha Map + Alpha Map + Map Channel - Drives opacity from a separate texture instead of the Albedo's alpha, handy when your Albedo has no alpha channel. White stays solid, black turns invisible. Pick the channel that holds the opacity, or leave it on Auto. Shown in Fade and Transparent modes.
  • Transparency - Fades the whole material out on one slider (0 solid, 1 invisible), so a single menu control can dissolve it. Works in Fade and Transparent; Opaque and Cutout ignore it.

UV Animation

  • UV Rotation - A static angular rotation applied to the surface UVs, in degrees.
  • UV Speed X - Horizontal UV scroll speed over time.
  • UV Speed Y - Vertical UV scroll speed over time. Combine with Speed X for diagonal flow.

NORMAL MAPS

Section 2 of 12

Macro normal map and the secondary micro-detail normal.

  • Normal Map - Tangent-space normal map for macro surface detail (leather grain, latex pores).
  • Enable Micro Detail - Turns on the secondary detail-normal layer.
  • Micro Detail Map - A secondary detail normal map for fine pores or hex-grid patterns layered over the macro surface.
  • Normal Strength - Intensity multiplier for the main normal map. Higher values deepen the perceived relief.
  • Detail Strength - Intensity of the micro-detail normal.
  • Detail UV Tiling - Independent tiling for the detail map so micro-texture can repeat at a different scale than the main UVs.
  • Card Normal Smoothing + Smoothing Pivot - For hair cards, fur planes and other flat pieces: bends the lighting normals outward from the pivot so the whole shape lights as one volume instead of a stack of separate cards. Set the pivot to the centre of the shape, in object space.

Bent Normals

Bake a bent normal map next to your AO in Substance, Marmoset or Blender and drop it here. Ambient light then follows the shape of the surface instead of washing over it, so crevices stay dark and forms read solid. Off by default.

  • Enable - Turns the bent-normal path on.
  • Map - The bent normal map.
  • Ambient Shaping - How strongly the bent normal shapes the ambient light falling on the surface.
  • Reflection Shaping - How strongly it shapes what the surface reflects from its surroundings.

SURFACE

Section 3 of 12

The packed PBR map, its channel routing, reflection / specular masks, and surface shaping.

Packed PBR Map

A single RGBA texture keeps VRAM efficient. The channel pickers below tell the shader which channel holds which property, so Poiyomi / Substance / Marmoset packings drop in without re-authoring. Default packing is R Metallic, G AO, B Displacement / Height, A Smoothness.

  • Packed PBR Mask - The texture slot for the packed map.
  • Global Metallic - Multiplies the packed map's metallic channel (0 = fully non-metallic regardless of the map, 1 = use the map as authored). Defaults to 0 because latex is a dielectric.
  • Global Smoothness - Multiplies the packed map's smoothness channel (default 0.5), a quick way to dial the whole surface glossier or more matte.

Channel Mapping

Tell each PBR property which channel of the Packed PBR Mask to read. Leave it alone for a VixenWear-native map, or hit Mochie Setup to match a Mochie / Poiyomi map in one click.

  • Metallic Channel / Invert Metallic - Which channel supplies metalness, and a flip for sources stored inverted.
  • Smoothness Channel / Channel is Roughness - Which channel supplies smoothness; enable the invert when the channel holds roughness.
  • AO Channel - Which channel supplies ambient occlusion, or None to disable AO sampling.
  • Mochie Metallic Map Setup - One-click translation: maps a Mochie / Poiyomi "Metallic Maps" texture (R Metallic, G Smoothness, B Reflection Mask, A Specular Mask) onto the packed slot, sets the channels, disables AO, and enables the masks. Drop the Mochie map into Packed PBR Mask first.

Reflection & Specular Masks

Reads two more channels off the packed map: a Reflection Mask that dims what the surface reflects from the world (probe / environment specular, clearcoat env, Light Volume, LTCGI), and a Specular Mask that dims direct-light highlights. Matcaps keep their own masks and are unaffected. Channel defaults match Mochie packing (B reflection, A specular).

  • Enable Masks - Turns the reflection and specular masks on.
  • Reflection Mask - Channel, Invert and Strength for the environment / reflection mask.
  • Specular Mask - Channel, Invert and Strength for the direct-highlight mask.
  • Reflection From Mask - Turns the black-and-white Reflection Mask into real metallic reflections: white areas go shiny. The roughness is worked out from your base texture and normal map, so no separate smoothness map is needed. Off by default.
  • Reflection Roughness - How sharp those reflections read; lower for a mirror finish.

Surface Shaping

Height (sub-section) - Where the height data comes from. By default it reads a channel of the Packed PBR Mask (Packed Mask Channel). Turn on Use Separate Map to load a standalone Height Map instead, with its own Map Channel, the way Substance and Marmoset export it. Height drives Parallax, Displacement and Cavity.

  • Specular Occlusion - How much AO also occludes specular / reflections, preventing glow in cavities that should be shadowed.
  • Cavity + Cavity Radius + Cavity Gloss - Sharpens the fine detail in your height source: pores, seams and stitching sink into shadow while ridges catch the light. Radius picks how fine that detail is (small for grain and skin, larger for folds), and Cavity Gloss lets those creases read glossier or more matte. Needs a height source above.
  • Cavity Reflection Blur / Cavity Reflection Occlusion - Use the same cavity detail on the reflection: Blur roughens it in the creases so it is not a clean mirror, Occlusion dims it there. Both leave the raised areas sharp.
  • Parallax Depth - Depth of the adaptive raymarch that fakes height inside the surface. 0 disables parallax.
  • Displacement Strength - Real geometric height pushed along the normal from the height source (needs tessellation to have vertices to move).
  • Tessellation Detail - How densely DX11 hardware tessellation subdivides the mesh for displacement, from 0 (coarse / cheap) to 1 (dense / smoother). Distance and screen-size adaptive, and capped so it can't run away. Leave at 0 if you aren't using Displacement.

SHADING

Section 4 of 12

The lighting model and every world-lighting integration, grouped together. Light Volumes, LTCGI and Stage / VRSL are each fail-safe: they strip or no-op cleanly in worlds that lack the system.

Lighting

Rim, subsurface, anisotropy, transmission, and energy conservation. Most of these belong to the polish layer and are gated by POLISH > Enable. AO Strength and Shadow Hardness now live in Shadow Detail.

  • Rim Light Strength - Intensity of the Fresnel rim glow around the silhouette.
  • Rim Light Power - Tightness of the rim falloff. Higher values pull the rim into a thinner edge band.
  • Subsurface Strength - Strength of the subsurface scattering lobe for soft, skin-like light diffusion.
  • Subsurface Distance - Wraps light past the shadow terminator so SSS bleeds gently into the dark side.
  • Subsurface Power - Tightness of the back-scatter highlight when light passes through thin geometry toward the viewer.
  • Anisotropy - Stretches the specular highlight along the tangent (-1 = vertical, +1 = horizontal, 0 = isotropic) for real latex-pull highlights.
  • Anisotropy Rotation - Rotates the anisotropic tangent around the normal, aligning the highlight to the visible stretch rather than the UV unwrap.
  • Transmission Strength - Strength of light bleeding through thin parts (ears, fingers, latex membranes) from behind.
  • Transmission Distance - Beer-Lambert absorption depth; shorter distances tint and darken the transmitted light faster.
  • Transmission Falloff - Angular tightness of the back-light term as the light moves behind the surface.
  • Multi-Scatter Compensation - Filament / Frostbite multi-scatter compensation that adds back energy lost at high roughness, keeping bright metals from going dull.
  • Horizon Specular Occlusion - Fades reflections that surface detail would otherwise leak through the body at glancing angles, keeping reflection probes, Light Volumes and stage lighting from showing through. Full by default; lower toward 0 to relax it.
  • Specular Highlight Clamp - Caps how bright a direct point-light highlight can get, taming the harsh sparkle very glossy latex can throw under bright lights. Effectively off at its default; lower it to clamp harder.

Micro Lighting

Brightens the raised detail so peaks catch a little more light. It is the light-side companion to Micro Shading over in Shadow Detail, which darkens the creases.

  • Peak Light - Master strength of the peak highlight.
  • Scene Lights / Ambient / Light Volumes / LTCGI / VRSL - Trim the effect per lighting system, so you can lean on it in one and ease off in another.

Cavity Lighting

Makes the Cavity detail react to the light direction: raised areas catch the light and creases fall into shadow as the light rakes across, on top of the flat Cavity shading. Needs Cavity turned on in SURFACE.

  • Strength - Master strength of the directional cavity response.
  • Scene Lights / Ambient - Trim it separately for the scene lights and the ambient.

Shadow Detail

Occlusion and shadow, gathered in one place. AO Strength and Shadow Hardness always apply; the dedicated maps below stay off until you enable them and read through the Albedo sampler, so they cost nothing against the texture budget.

  • AO Strength - How strongly the surface map's packed AO channel darkens ambient and indirect light. 0 = off, 1 = full, higher deepens the shading in creases.
  • Shadow Hardness - Sharpness of the self-shadowing cast inside recessed seams by the parallax raymarch.

Micro Shading

Makes occlusion react to where the light is coming from, so creases go dark when the light rakes in from the side and open up when it hits head on. Without it, occlusion dims everything evenly and reads as dirt painted on.

  • Strength - Master strength of the directional occlusion.
  • Scene Lights / Ambient / Light Volumes / LTCGI / VRSL - Trim it per lighting system, so you can lean on it in the ambient and ease off under a stage rig.
  • Assumed Direction - Scene lights, ambient and Light Volumes tell the material which way their light arrives; LTCGI and VRSL do not, so pick what to assume for those two. Above suits ceiling panels and overhead rigs, Camera keys it to where you are looking from, and Surface keeps it flat and just deepens the creases.

Occlusion Map

  • Enable Occlusion Map - Turns on the dedicated ambient-occlusion map.
  • Occlusion Map + Occlusion Channel - A grayscale map, and which channel to read, that darkens ambient and indirect light in creases and cavities on top of any occlusion already packed into the surface map. White = no change.
  • Occlusion Strength - How strongly the map darkens the ambient / indirect lighting.

Baked Shadow Map

  • Enable Baked Shadow Map - Turns on the baked contact-shadow map.
  • Baked Shadow Map + Shadow Channel - A grayscale map, and which channel to read, painting regions that always read as shadowed - baked contact shadows under straps, seams and deep folds. Black = no shadow.
  • Shadow Strength - How dark the baked-shadow regions get in direct light.
  • Shadow Affects Ambient - How much the baked shadow also dims ambient / indirect light in those regions (0 = direct light only).

Light Volumes V3

The full VRC Light Volumes V3 stack.

Alongside baked volumes the material also receives Point Light Volumes - point lights, spot lights (parametric / attenuation-LUT / projected cookie), cubemap-tinted point lights, and quad area lights (the "TV GI" path: an area light driven by a video RenderTexture), plus per-light EVSM shadows. All of it projects into the same L1 spherical-harmonics path and rides the controls below. These lights only appear in worlds running the LV V3 runtime; older or no-LV worlds fall back to deringed light probes.

  • Enable Light Volumes - Runtime gate for the whole Light Volumes path, so VRCFury can toggle it without a recompile. Off by default.
  • Light Volumes Intensity - Overall contribution of Light Volumes lighting.
  • Light Volumes Specular Mix - How much Light Volume data feeds the base specular lobe.
  • Light Volumes Clearcoat Specular - How much Light Volume data feeds the clearcoat-specific specular.
  • Clearcoat Specular Tightness - Shrinks the clearcoat's Light Volume specular lobe so a bright point or area light reads as a tight glint instead of a broad wash (0 = soft / wide, 1 = tightest).
  • Specular (Dominant Mode) - Uses the dominant light direction instead of the full L1 reconstruction for cheaper specular.
  • Point Light Volume Specular (Per-Light) - Opt-in: gives each point, spot and area light its own size-aware specular highlight for sharper, more accurate glints on glossy latex, instead of deriving point-light specular from the blended light direction. Off by default.
  • Point Light Volume Shading - How softly point / spot / area lights wrap across the surface normal (higher = softer falloff).
  • Shadow / Volumetric Normal Stability - How geometric the normal is that drives V3's point / spot / area-light shading and their baked (EVSM) shadows. At 1 (default) a stable geometric normal is used, so parallax, detail normals, wetness, and drips no longer smear the new shadowcasting and volumetrics; lower it to let surface bump feed back into the point-light shadow mask.
  • Normal Bias - Offsets the sample point along the normal to fix light bleed at sharp edges.
  • Position Offset - Manual world-space sample offset for thin or sleeve geometry that samples the wrong cell.
  • Additive-Only Mode - Adds Light Volume light on top of Unity's probe baseline instead of replacing it.
  • Use Deringed Probes (Bakery L1, opt-in) - Opt-in Bakery L1 deringed-probe fallback for worlds without Light Volumes.

LTCGI V2

LTCGI area lighting, with per-lobe mode and color tints.

When a world bakes its LTCGI into the Light Volumes atlas, the material's direct LTCGI sampling is skipped automatically so the surface is not lit twice. In every other world the controls below apply directly.

  • Enable LTCGI - Runtime gate for the LTCGI path, off by default; VRCFury can flip it without a recompile.
  • LTCGI Intensity - Master contribution of LTCGI area lighting.
  • LTCGI Specular Mix - Area-light specular contribution, dialed independently.
  • LTCGI Diffuse Mix - Area-light diffuse contribution, dialed independently.
  • LTCGI Mode - Which lobes the area lights feed: Both, Diffuse Only, or Specular Only.
  • LTCGI Diffuse Color - Tints the LTCGI diffuse contribution.
  • LTCGI Specular Color - Tints the LTCGI specular contribution.

Stage Lighting & VRSL

VRSL DMX routing, geo-warping, color hijack, and the additive GI stage wash.

VRSL Stage Hijack links this material directly to world-space DMX buffers. When stage light is detected it overrides the native AudioLink color arrays and physically warps tessellated vertex positions to match stage configurations in real time, mimicking a Moving Head fixture. It contributes nothing in worlds with no DMX node.

  • Enable VRSL DMX Link - Master toggle that connects the material to the world's VRSL DMX buffers.
  • DMX Base Channel - Which fixture sector / base channel this material reads from the DMX universe.
  • Override Strength - How strongly the stage data takes over the surface (0 = no override, up = full hijack).
  • Pan/Tilt Geo-Warping - How far the tessellated mesh physically warps to follow the fixture's DMX Pan/Tilt values.
  • Color Hijack - Lerps the AudioLink color toward live DMX RGB so the stage can wash the palette without disabling FFT-driven color movement.

GI Wash Overrides

Separate from the hijack above: instead of overriding emission, the GI wash spills the DMX fixtures' colour onto the material as real additive light (the stage lighting you up). It reads the same DMX channels as the Color Hijack.

  • Intensity - Master strength of the additive stage wash. Above 0 enables it.
  • Specular Mix - Fresnel-weighted specular highlight from the wash.
  • Color Saturation - Desaturates the wash toward luma so it tints the surface rather than repainting your design.

DECALS

Section 5 of 12

Place up to four logos, stickers, brand graphics or tattoos on the latex. Each layer is positioned right in the Scene view: select the material on your mesh and drag the on-screen gizmo handles to move, scale and rotate the decal. All four layers share one texture sampler, so they cost nothing against the texture budget, and the whole section stays off until you enable it.

Setup

  • Enable - Master switch for the whole decal section. Off by default, so existing materials are untouched.
  • Decal Masks (RGBA) - One optional packed texture whose Red, Green, Blue and Alpha channels can each limit where Decal 0, 1, 2 and 3 appear. Leave it white to let decals show wherever their placement allows.

Per Decal (0 - 3)

  • Enable - Turns this individual layer on.
  • Decal - The image to place (logo, sticker, tattoo, and so on).
  • Tint - Colour multiplied over the decal; the colour's alpha sets the layer's overall opacity.
  • Position / Scale / Rotation - Where the decal sits, how big it is, and its angle. Easiest to set by dragging the gizmo in the Scene view. Rotation Speed slowly spins the decal over time.
  • Side Offset - Nudges the left, right, bottom and top edges independently to stretch or crop the decal box.
  • UV Source - Which UV layout to place the decal on (the main set, or a secondary set if your mesh has one).
  • Mask Channel - Which channel of the Decal Masks texture limits this layer.
  • Coverage From - What decides where the decal shows. Texture Alpha uses the image's own transparency and is the normal choice. Luminance uses how light the image is, Luminance Inverted uses how dark it is, which is what painted shading or muscle definition on a white background needs so those drop straight in from another shader. Ignore Alpha covers the whole decal box, for images whose transparency is unusable.
  • Color Blend - How the decal combines with the latex underneath: Normal, Darken, Multiply, Lighten, Screen, Add, Subtract or Overlay.
  • Strength - Makes the decal glow, for neon logos or lit signage.
  • Smoothness Offset - Makes the decal read glossier (positive) or more matte (negative) than the surrounding latex, so a printed sticker can stand apart from the shine around it.
  • Mirror UV / Symmetry - Flip the decal, restrict it to one side, or mirror it across the body's centre for symmetric placement.
  • Tiled - Off keeps a single decal clamped to its box; on repeats it across the surface.

POLISH

Section 6 of 12

The polish master gate, clearcoat, and thin film.

Enable is the master gate for the whole polish lighting layer (clearcoat, thin film, plus the SSS / transmission / anisotropy / rim / multi-scatter terms over in SHADING > Lighting). Off collapses the material to a flat GGX base.

  • Enable - Turns the whole polish stack on or off.
  • Mask - Optional grayscale mask scoping the polish layer to painted regions.
  • Mask Channel - Which channel of the mask to read.
  • Clearcoat Strength - Intensity of the dielectric clear coat lobe layered over the base.
  • Clearcoat Smoothness - Smoothness of the coat (1 = mirror-sharp gloss).
  • Specular Anti-Aliasing - Toksvig-style geometric spec AA that roughens the normal from screen-space derivative variance, killing spec sparkle at mid-distance.
  • Clearcoat Flattening - Blends the coat's normal toward the geometric normal so the glossy film reads smooth over rough underlying texture.
  • Clearcoat Tint - Color cast of the coat reflection. White is neutral; colored tints attenuate the under-layer per channel for oil-on-water looks.
  • Clearcoat Reflectivity - Base Fresnel reflectance of the coat. 0.04 reproduces a standard dielectric exactly; higher values brighten the grazing-angle reflection.
  • Thin Film Strength - Strength of the interference rainbow across the coat.
  • Thin Film Thickness - Optical thickness of the thin film, which sets which wavelengths shift and therefore the iridescent hue.
  • Cavity Gloss Blur / Cavity Gloss Occlusion - Use the Cavity surface detail to break up the clearcoat reflection: Blur roughens the mirror in the creases, Occlusion dims it there. Needs Cavity turned on in SURFACE.

SPECIAL EFFECTS

Section 7 of 12

The extra effect layers: the soaked / wet look with animated run-off and 3D drips, the melting goo, procedural glitter, an animated flipbook overlay, a true infinity mirror, a living inner-cosmos volume, and effects that react to where the material is being viewed from. The VixForge Toon shader carries everything here except the wet and goo effects, which are liquid-latex tricks that do not fit a toon surface.

Wet Effects

The soaked look, animated run-off rivulets, and clear 3D drips.

Master & Soaked Look

Soaked Look (Just Out of the Pool) - Soaks the masked area: darkens the latex, drives reflections to a near-mirror water film, adds a dielectric Fresnel sheen, and flattens micro-detail. Film Sheen rides on the clearcoat, so keep the Polish layer enabled for the strongest wet highlight.

  • Enable Drip - Master toggle for the wet / run-off layer.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Grayscale mask (and channel) choosing where the surface is wet and where drips form.
  • Wetness - Overall amount of the wet effect.
  • Darkening - How much the soaked area darkens, simulating water absorption.
  • Smoothness - Smoothness of the water film (toward a near-mirror reflection).
  • Film Sheen - Strength of the dielectric Fresnel sheen riding on the clearcoat.
  • Normal Flatten - Flattens micro-detail under the film so the wet area reads slick.

Run-Off Rivulets

Animated vertical streaks of water running off, layered on top of the soak. Set Streak Strength to 0 for a still, evenly-soaked look.

  • Streak Density - Number of rivulet columns across the surface.
  • Streak Thinness - Width of each streak; higher values produce narrower streaks.
  • Streak Coverage - How much of the masked area the rivulets occupy (shared with the 3D drips).
  • Flow Speed - Vertical scroll speed of the run-off (shared with the 3D drips).
  • Streak Strength - Visual intensity of the streaks.
  • Streak Bump - How much each streak perturbs the normal for a raised, refractive bead.

Clear 3D Drips (Geometry, PC only)

Real water droplets emitted by a geometry shader: they swell on downward-facing wet areas, form a neck, pinch off, and fall away as free geometry, tinted to the Clearcoat Tint. PC only - it uses a geometry stage, so it is not present on Quest.

  • Droplet Amount - How many droplets spawn across the wet area.
  • Droplet Scale - Approximate droplet diameter.
  • Droplet Glassiness - How clear / refractive the droplet looks versus a milky bead.
  • Droplet Fall Length - How far a drop falls before it fades out.

Physics & Collision

Sway adds surface-tension wobble to falling drops. Surface Slide makes an attached drop run down along the body before it detaches. Floor Splat pins drops to the shared world floor (the Goo "Ground / Floor Height") and spreads them into a fading puddle.

  • Sway / Wobble - Surface-tension wobble and breeze on falling drops.
  • Surface Slide - How far an attached drop runs along the body before detaching.
  • Droplet Floor Splat - Pins landed drops to the shared world floor and spreads them into a fading puddle.

Goo Effects

Gravity-aligned vertex melt that mimics runny / melting latex, with sway and floor collision.

Runs in the displacement stage and benefits from tessellation on the base shader (more verts = smoother strands). Extreme stretch can be frustum-culled when the body is off-screen unless the mesh bounds (or an Anchor Override) are expanded.

  • Enable - Master toggle for the melting-sag system.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Grayscale mask (and channel) scoping where the latex melts.
  • Sag Distance - Master intensity of the melt.
  • Stretch Distance - How far the goo sags, in world units; dramatically extends strand length.
  • Strand Variation - Procedural FBM noise so tendrils range from uniform (0) to wildly uneven (1).
  • Tendril Scale - Spatial scale of the tendril pattern.
  • Flow Speed - How fast the melt animates / flows over time.
  • Underside Bias - Biases melting toward downward-facing surfaces where latex would actually drip.
  • Melt To Ground - Pulls the goo down toward the world ground plane so strands can reach the floor regardless of avatar height.
  • Ground Height - The world-space Y of your floor (usually 0), shared by both the goo and the drip floor splat.

Physics & Collision

Sway gives the tendrils a gentle pendulum swing. Surface Follow flows goo along the body instead of sinking through it. Floor Collision clamps the melt to the Ground / Floor Height, and Floor Pooling spreads landed strands sideways into a puddle.

  • Sway Amount - Pendulum swing magnitude of the tendrils.
  • Sway Speed - Rate of the sway.
  • Surface Follow - How much goo flows along the body instead of through it.
  • Floor Collision - Clamps the melt to the floor height instead of passing through it.
  • Floor Pooling - Spreads landed strands sideways into a puddle.

Glitter

Tiny flakes scattered across the material, each tilted its own way, so they catch the light one at a time and twinkle as you or the light moves. They are generated in the shader, so there is no texture to make and no resolution to run out of. Once flakes get too small on screen to draw individually they settle into a smooth sheen, so glitter never turns into crawling noise at a distance.

The Flakes

  • Enable - Master switch for the glitter layer, off by default.
  • Anchor - UV follows your unwrap, so flakes stretch where the UVs stretch. World Space keeps every flake the same real size across the whole avatar, which is usually what you want for an all-over finish.
  • Density / Flake Size - How many flakes, and how big each one is.
  • Coverage - What share of the surface gets a flake at all. The number is honest: 0.35 puts one on about 35% of the possible spots.
  • Scatter - How randomly the flakes tilt. Low keeps them lying flat with the surface so they flash together, high sends them everywhere for a rougher, busier sparkle.
  • Sharpness - How briefly each flake flashes, from a broad shimmer to hard, quick pinpricks. It changes the character of the sparkle, not how much of it you get, so both ends of the slider are safe to use.
  • Colour + Tint - Tint gives every flake one colour. Iridescent gives each flake its own hue for a holographic finish, with Rainbow Amount setting how far the colours spread. ColorChord takes the colours from the music.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Limits the flakes to part of the material.
  • AudioLink Band / AudioLink Amount - Makes the flakes flare and multiply on the beat. Shown only when AudioLink is enabled.

Glitter Lighting

The flakes are lit like a real surface rather than painted on, so they answer to whatever is lighting the room. Lit By chooses which lighting that is.

  • Lit By - Natural uses the world's ordinary lights and reflection probes. Light Volumes, LTCGI and VRSL each tie the glitter to that one system, so it lights up in worlds running that system and stays dark in worlds that do not. Everything takes light from all of them at once. Always Lit ignores lighting completely, so the sparkle shows even in a pitch black room.
  • Brightness / Ambient Sparkle - The flash from direct lights, and the flash from the surrounding room. Turn Ambient Sparkle up if the world has no strong light and the glitter looks dead.
  • Minimum Light - Stops the flakes going completely dark in an unlit corner. Leave it at 0 for glitter that behaves like a real surface.
  • Take Light Colour - Whether the flakes take on the colour of the light hitting them, or keep their own Tint and use only the light's brightness. Turn it down to keep your colour under coloured stage lighting.
  • Over Clearcoat - Where the flakes sit relative to the shine from Polish. At 1 they sit on top and cut through it, so glitter stays crisp on a glossy material. At 0 they sit underneath like flakes in real car paint, dimmed by the coat and fading out at glancing angles. Only appears when Polish is on, and it makes no difference to materials without a clearcoat.

Viewer Context

The material can tell where it is being drawn and who is looking at it, and react to that on its own. Nothing here needs an animator, a menu or a parameter, and everyone sees their own version at the same moment.

  • Enable - Master switch, off by default.
  • Show - Only in Mirrors hides the effect from everyone else while you still see it in a mirror, and Never in Mirrors does the reverse. Only in Photos and Never in Photos catch the in-game camera and screenshots, so you can add a detail that appears only in someone's photo, or keep an effect out of every picture taken of you. Only in VR and Only on Desktop split by headset and flatscreen.
  • Seen By - In VR you can send the effect to one eye only. There is no other way to get that look.
  • Distance + Near Distance / Far Distance - Fades the effect in as someone comes closer, or as they back away, set in metres.
  • Face-On Only - The effect only shows when the surface is turned toward the viewer, so it hides at glancing angles.
  • Hidden Level - What the effect drops to when the conditions are not met. 0 hides it completely, higher values just dim it.
  • Affects - Point it at your emission, your decals, or both.
  • Reaction Glow - An extra colour that rides along with everything above. Pair it with Distance for a material that lights up when somebody leans in.

Distances and angles are measured from the real viewer, and mirrors are handled properly, so a reflection reacts to where the mirror is looking from rather than lighting up wrongly.

Flipbook

An animated overlay that plays across the surface, then blends over the material like a decal. Drop a GIF or a set of images onto Frames and it bakes into one flipbook; or point it at a sprite-sheet laid out in a grid. Off by default, so existing materials are untouched.

  • Enable - Master switch for the flipbook layer.
  • Is Spritesheet - Off reads an imported flipbook (a GIF or image sequence baked into one array, dropped onto Frames). On reads a single Sheet texture laid out in a grid, where you set Columns, Rows and Frame Count.
  • Speed (FPS) - Playback rate in frames per second.
  • Tint - Colour multiplied over the flipbook.
  • Position / Scale / Rotation / Side Offset - Where the flipbook sits, how big it is, its angle, and independent edge nudges. Easiest to set by dragging the gizmo in the Scene view. Rotation Speed slowly spins it over time.
  • UV Source - Which UV layout to place it on.
  • Coverage From - What decides where the flipbook shows: the image's Texture Alpha (the normal choice), its Luminance, Luminance Inverted (for painted art on a white background), or Ignore Alpha to cover the whole box.
  • Blend - How it combines with the latex underneath: Normal, Darken, Multiply, Lighten, Screen, Add, Subtract or Overlay.
  • Opacity - Fades the whole overlay in and out.
  • Mirror UV / Symmetry / Tiled - Flip it, restrict it to one side, mirror it across the body centre, or repeat it across the surface.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Limits the flipbook to part of the material.

Infinity Mirror

A true infinity mirror. The surface reads as glass over a light that reflects away into the distance, a receding tunnel of copies that shifts as you move around it. Off by default. Idea and special thanks to Ghostpaw.

  • Enable - Master switch for the effect.
  • Pattern + Pattern Texture - What sits at the bottom of the tunnel: a Ring of Lights, a Dot Grid, or your own Texture.
  • Glow Colour - HDR colour of the lights.
  • Reflections - How many copies deep the tunnel goes.
  • Depth / Fade Per Layer / Recession - How far back the tunnel reaches, how quickly each copy dims, and how fast the copies shrink into the distance.
  • Vanishing Point - Where the tunnel recedes toward on the surface.
  • Rainbow Depth - Shifts the hue deeper into the tunnel for a spectral falloff.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Limits the effect to part of the material.
  • AudioLink Band / AudioLink Amount - Pulses the glow with a band. Shown only when AudioLink is enabled.

Inner Cosmos

The surface becomes a thin window onto a living volume of energy with real depth: look into it from any angle and it parallaxes and swirls like you are peering into a contained galaxy, and it never repeats. Off by default. Original effect designed by Claude (Opus 4.8) for VixForge.

  • Enable - Master switch for the effect.
  • Deep Colour + Bright Colour - The two colours that set the mood, from the far depths to the bright core.
  • Brightness / Density / Contrast - Overall glow, how cloudy the volume is, and how hard the light and dark separate.
  • Swirl / Rotation / Spin - The spiral, a static angle, and a continuous rotation over time.
  • Scale / Warp / Depth / Flow Speed - Size of the pattern, how much it twists, how far into the surface it reaches, and how fast it drifts.
  • Stars - Turn up for a night-sky sprinkle of stars.
  • Quality - Raise only if you see banding.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Limits the effect to part of the material.
  • AudioLink Band / AudioLink Amount - Pulses the brightness with a band. Shown only when AudioLink is enabled.

Living Energy (AudioLink)

A separate layer that only wakes up with AudioLink and turns the whole volume into cosmic energy that moves to the music. Shown only when AudioLink is enabled.

  • Living Energy + Reaction Band - Turns the layer on and picks the band it listens to.
  • Comet Tails - Streams tails off the stars along the way the galaxy spins.
  • Arm Whip - Whips the spiral arms out on the beat.
  • Cosmic Breath - Breathes the whole field in and out.
  • Depth Spectrum - Lights the depths bass to treble, near to far.
  • Flare Colour - HDR colour for the beat flares.

OUTLINES

Section 8 of 12

Two stacked outlines around the silhouette, an inner and an outer, each with its own colour, glow, width, scrolling texture and AudioLink boost.

Each outline draws as a shell ahead of the main pass. Width is a real thickness that grows the line as you raise it and shrinks naturally as the camera moves away, like a real object. Both are off by default, so a material without outlines costs nothing.

Outline 1

The inner line around the silhouette.

  • Enable - Turns the inner outline on.
  • Color - Solid colour of the line.
  • Emission - HDR glow colour added on top for a neon edge.
  • Width - Real thickness of the line; grows as you raise it and shrinks with distance.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Optional mask (and channel) restricting the outline to painted regions; set the channel to None for an even line all the way round.
  • Texture + Texture Scroll - Flow a pattern through the line, and scroll it in X / Y over time.
  • AudioLink Band + AudioLink Emission Boost - Which band drives the line, and how strongly it pumps the glow.

Outline 2 (Outer)

A second line sitting just outside Outline 1, for a two-tone edge, say a coloured inner line with a black outer one. It has the same controls as Outline 1 and always sits outside it, so its Width adds on top.

  • Enable - Turns the outer outline on.
  • Color, Emission, Width, Mask + Mask Channel, Texture + Texture Scroll, AudioLink Band + AudioLink Emission Boost - The same controls as Outline 1, for the outer line.

EMISSIONS

Section 9 of 12

Five stacked emission layers, the Pathing effect, and a multi-region colour mask. Every layer's AudioLink controls sit inline with that layer and appear only when AudioLink is enabled.

Emission Exposure & Layer 1

  • Exposure (All Layers) - Extra exposure multiplier over every layer for fine bloom control.
  • Color + Map - HDR tint / intensity and the texture defining where layer 1 emits.
  • Strength / Hue Shift / Hue Cycle Speed - Brightness and colour for this layer on their own sliders, so a single radial puppet can drive either one straight from your menu without animating an HDR colour across four curves. Hue Cycle Speed rotates the colour on its own with no animator at all.
  • Layer 1 AudioLink Band / Amplitude / Color Blend - Which AudioLink band drives layer 1, how strongly it pulses, and how much AudioLink's colour replaces the emission colour. Shown only when AudioLink is enabled.

Emission Layers 2-5

  • Enable Layer 2 (and layers 3, 4, 5) - Each adds a fully independent extra emission layer, off by default.
  • Color + Emission Map 2 + Mask Channel - HDR tint, texture, and mask channel for each layer (layers 3-5 mirror these controls).
  • Strength / Hue Shift / Hue Cycle Speed - Every layer gets its own set, so you can put a brightness dial on one layer and a colour wheel on another.
  • Layer AudioLink Band / Amplitude - Which AudioLink band reacts on each layer and how strongly it modulates that layer's brightness, so you can route bass to one and treble to another. Shown only when AudioLink is enabled.

Pathing

Runs light along a path: chase lights, loading bars, energy running down a limb. Paint the path into a texture as a black-to-white ramp and the glow travels from the black end to the white end. All four channels are read, so one texture carries four separate paths.

  • Pathing - Master switch for the effect, off by default.
  • Path Gradient + Panning - The black-to-white ramp texture, and how it drifts over time.
  • Source - Read the path from the gradient texture, or pick UV X or UV Y for a straight sweep with no texture at all.
  • Blend - Add glows on top of your other layers, Replace takes over where the path is lit, and Multiply tints them.

Per Path (Red, Green, Blue, Alpha)

  • Type - Fill wipes along the path, Path sends one pulse through it, Loop repeats seamlessly, and Dashed marches a dotted line.
  • Color - HDR colour for this path.
  • Speed / Start Offset / Width / Softness - How fast it moves, where it starts, how long the lit part is, and how soft its edges are.
  • Gap - Spacing between dashes, for the Dashed type.
  • Steps - Snaps the movement into that many jumps for a chunky, mechanical feel. Leave at 0 for smooth.
  • AudioLink - Brightness pulses with a band. Beat Travel moves the path to the beat instead of the clock. Waveform and Autocorrelator paint the live audio along the path, so it reads like a scope trace. ColorChord colours it with the notes actually being played. Shown only when AudioLink is enabled.

Multi-Region Color Mask (RGB Zones)

Each channel acts as an independent zone mask. The zone tint multiplies into the albedo (white = no change), and the emission boost adds zone-colored glow on top, letting you paint hard-edged feature zones without a second material.

  • Multi-Region Color Mask - Turns on the three-zone color mask.
  • Region Mask - The mask texture whose R, G, and B channels define three independent zones.
  • Red Zone Tint / Red Zone Emission Boost - Albedo tint and added glow for the red zone.
  • Green Zone Tint / Green Zone Emission Boost - Albedo tint and added glow for the green zone.
  • Blue Zone Tint / Blue Zone Emission Boost - Albedo tint and added glow for the blue zone.

MATCAPS

Section 10 of 12

Up to five stacked material-capture layers with a shared projection setting, plus a Car Paint look. Each layer has its own Tiling & Movement and Offsets sub-sections, and can be frozen to read as part of the material rather than a sliding reflection.

Global Settings

  • Projection - Blends matcap sampling between camera-aligned and mesh-aligned. 0 = pure camera-aligned (sliding / reflective), 1 = pure mesh-UV-aligned (glued to the geometry). Higher values pin the matcap to the surface and reduce the sliding feeling as the camera moves. Applies to every layer; for a per-layer lock, use that layer's Freeze instead.

Car Paint

A layered paint look. The Base and Flip colours travel with your view for a pearlescent flip-flop, metallic flake sparkles at grazing angles, and Gloss sets the wet clearcoat shine. A matcap layer with its Blend Mode set to CarPaint feeds into it. Off by default.

  • Enable + Amount - Turns the look on and sets how strongly it takes over.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Paints the look onto part of the material only.
  • Base Colour + Flip Colour - The two colours of the flip-flop, seen face-on and at a grazing angle.
  • Flip Amount + Flip Falloff - How much the colour shifts toward the Flip Colour, and how tightly that shift hugs the grazing edge.
  • Flake Colour + Flake Sparkle + Flake Density + Flake Sharpness - Colour, strength, count and crispness of the metallic flake.
  • Gloss Smoothness + Gloss Metallic - The wet clearcoat shine over the paint.

Matcap 1

  • Enable - Turns layer 1 on.
  • Texture - The material-capture sphere texture.
  • Mask + Mask Channel - Mask, and which channel, restricting the layer to painted regions.
  • Tint - Colour tint applied to the matcap.
  • Rotation - Rotates the matcap sample.
  • Intensity - Brightness of the layer, now up to 6 for a strong, over-driven glow.
  • Blend Mode - How the layer combines: Add, Replace, Multiply, or CarPaint, which drives the layer through the Car Paint group above.
  • Opacity - Fades the whole layer in and out over the base. Pair it with Multiply or Replace to cover the base and read as latex; Add only lays highlights on top. Full by default.
  • Lighting Mix - How much scene lighting modulates the layer versus reading it as a pure unlit overlay.

Tiling & Movement (sub-section) - Freeze stops the matcap sliding as the camera moves, so it reads as baked-in detail that turns naturally with the avatar (0 = classic reflective matcap). Tiling repeats the matcap around its centre. Scroll / Animate gives continuous motion: Pan X / Y move it over time (Pan Y = up / down), Spin rotates it, Speed scales the animation.

Offsets (sub-section) - Position Offset nudges the matcap statically: Offset X moves it side to side, Offset Y up and down.

Matcap 2

A second matcap layer with the same controls (including its own Tiling & Movement and Offsets sub-sections). Tip: drop a red/blue/black region mask into both Layer 1's and Layer 2's Mask, set Layer 1's channel to R and Layer 2's to B, and each colour zone shows a different matcap.

Matcap 3, 4 & 5

Three more matcap layers, each with the identical control set - texture, mask and channel, tint, rotation, intensity (up to 6), blend mode, opacity, lighting mix, plus its own Tiling & Movement (with Freeze) and Offsets sub-sections - for additional stacked zones. All off by default.

RENDERING

Section 12 of 12

Culling, blend and stencil overrides. The Rendering Mode up in BASE already sets sensible values, so leave these alone unless you need a specific result.

Models must be triangles. If yours looks torn into triangles or shredded, select the model file, open its Model Import Settings, turn off Keep Quads and hit Apply.

  • Cull - Which side of each face is drawn. Back suits most models; switch to Off for flat single-sided pieces like hair cards, skirts or wings that need to show from both sides.
  • Depth Test - How the surface tests against the depth buffer. Leave at the default unless you are layering effects.
  • Color Mask - Which colour channels the material is allowed to write.
  • Offset Factor + Offset Units - Depth-bias the surface to fix z-fighting against a coincident mesh.
  • Z Clip - Clips geometry at the near / far planes. On by default.
  • Alpha To Coverage - Smooths hard cutout edges using MSAA coverage.
  • Flip Backface Normals - Flips the lighting normal on back faces, so single-sided pieces light correctly when Cull is Off.
  • Ignore Fog - Excludes the material from the world fog.

Blending - How the material combines with what is already on screen. Rendering Mode sets these, so change them only for a specific blend.

  • RGB Blend Op + RGB Source + RGB Destination - The colour blend equation and its two factors.
  • Alpha Blend Op + Alpha Source + Alpha Destination - The same, for the alpha channel.

Stencil - Stencil-buffer routing for masks, portals and effects that read or write the stencil.

  • Reference - The stencil value this material compares and writes.
  • Comparison - The test against the buffer that decides whether the pixel draws.
  • Pass + Fail + Z Fail - What happens to the stored stencil value on each outcome.
  • Read Mask + Write Mask - Which bits of the stencil the test reads and the write touches.

VixForge Toon

Companion Shader

A cel and anime sibling to Latex Ultra. It shares every section on this page, and adds control over how light falls off the surface.

VixForge Toon carries the same toolkit as Latex Ultra - PBR base, matcaps, emissions, decals, outlines, AudioLink, Light Volumes, LTCGI and VRSL - so every section above applies to it unchanged. Two things differ: it adds the Shading Options block below, and it drops Wet Effects and Goo Effects, which are liquid-latex tricks that do not fit a toon surface. Reach for Toon when you want a stylised look, and for Latex Ultra when you want a physically based latex finish. Toon can also do a fully realistic pass, so you can stylise the body and keep realistic boots on the same avatar.

Shading Options

  • Enable - Turns the stylised falloff on. Off, or with Style set to Realistic, the surface lights like a standard PBR material.
  • Style - Picks how light falls off:
    • Ramp Texture - Reads a gradient you supply, unlit on the left to lit on the right. Leave it white for a clean two-tone.
    • Cel Steps - Posterises the light into flat bands.
    • Soft - A smooth, rounded falloff.
    • Hard - One crisp terminator line.
    • Realistic - No stylising, the full physical response, for realistic parts on a Toon material.
  • Gradient - The ramp texture, read left (unlit) to right (lit). Shown for the Ramp Texture style.
  • Offset - Shifts where the ramp is read. Shown for the Ramp Texture style.
  • Count - The number of flat bands, 2 to 8. Shown for the Cel Steps style.
  • Light Wrap - The Realistic style's own softening of the terminator, the way skin, fabric and fur do, without losing energy.

Shared Falloff Controls

  • Edge Softness - How sharp the band edges are, for the Ramp Texture and Cel Steps styles. Low is crisp, high blurs the step.
  • Light Wrap - Pulls light further around the surface so more of it reads as lit and the terminator softens. Available on every style but Realistic. 0 keeps the plain terminator.
