Development & AI Transparency

The Vixens Toolbox is built to an Enterprise standard. To maintain that velocity as a SOLO Sr. Software Engineer AI is leveraged as a rapid-prototyping assistant. But make no mistake: the foundation of this ecosystem is built on ruthless, painstaking engineering. Here is the raw data on how our tools are built, grounded in real-world professional metrics.

100% Hand-Reviewed & Surgically Tested

We do not blindly copy-paste code. EVERY single line of C# and UdonSharp that enters the Vixens Toolbox ecosystem undergoes extreme scrutiny. Every custom inspector and every UI Toolkit element is mapped out, stress-tested inside live Unity environments, and debugged by hand. AI generates boilerplate; human oversight guarantees it scales, holds together, and stays maintainable.

The Deployed AI Stack

Our core development engine is Anthropic's Claude, driven agentically through Claude Code (Claude Opus 4.8). It is never used as a blind code generator. It runs inside a retrieval-grounded workflow that indexes the real Unity engine source, the VRChat SDK, and our own shader code, so the design is checked against ground truth instead of guessed. Domain skills enforce hard platform limits up front (for example UdonSharp's compile constraints), a documentation index of our own codebase keeps every edit surgical, and a persistent memory layer carries project conventions across sessions. Every line it proposes still passes through human review before it ships.

Measurable Engineering Statistics

Internal tracking shows an 80% reduction in time spent writing boilerplate UI Toolkit schemas, and a 60% faster turnaround on Fixing Real Issues That Ai Otherwise Can't. The hundreds of hours saved are redirected straight into rigorous QA, edge-case testing, and deep-dive reverse engineering of Unity APIs.

How We Actually Use AI

Four concrete roles AI plays in the VixForge pipeline. None of them replace engineering judgment, they remove the drudgery around it so the hard problems get the attention.

Accelerate Development

AI writes the parts that are purely mechanical: UI Toolkit schemas, editor-window plumbing, serialization boilerplate, and repetitive inspector wiring. Work that used to eat an afternoon of typing lands in minutes, so engineering hours go to design and edge cases instead of busywork.

Rapidly Prototype

Before we commit to a feature, we spin up throwaway implementations to test whether an approach is even viable, a decimation strategy, an inspector layout, a shader control, and iterate on them fast. Cheap experiments up front mean the version that ships is the one that actually earned its place.

Reverse-Engineer & Ground

The workflow indexes the real Unity C# reference, the VRChat SDK, and even Blender's mesh internals, then reads the actual implementation. Behaviour around culling bounds, PhysBones, and mesh data is derived from source, not hallucinated from a model's memory. When the AI is unsure, it looks it up rather than inventing an answer.

Hand-Review & Verify

Every proposed line is read, stress-tested inside a live Unity project, and verified end to end before it ships. AI proposes; the engineer decides. This is the non-negotiable step that keeps the toolbox stable across radically different VRChat projects.

Effects Designed With AI

A handful of the shader effects started as an AI design rather than a human one. They were specified, built and tuned by Claude working inside the VixForge codebase, then reviewed, tested and shipped by us. We would rather say so here than quietly take the credit.

Designed by Claude, shipped by us

These five live under SPECIAL EFFECTS in Latex Ultra, Toon, Clothing Pro and Fur Pro. Each one exists because AudioLink publishes data that no avatar shader had ever read, and the design work of turning that data into something worth looking at was AI's.

  • Inner Cosmos. A raymarched volume with real depth, so the surface reads as a window onto a contained galaxy.
  • Sonar. Works out where the music physically is in the room and sends a wave out from it across you.
  • Chromatic Bloom. Lays the twelve notes across the material so a chord reads as a chord, not one lump of brightness.
  • Note Swarm. Scatters ColorChord's per-note lights over you as drifting blobs that follow the harmony.
  • Spectrum Ribbon. Draws the last second and a half of the track onto you as a moving readout.

Credit where it is owed elsewhere too: the Infinity Mirror was Ghostpaw's idea, and every one of these effects is built on AudioLink by llealloo, cnlohr, lox9973, pema99 and float3.
