Ecosystem Superiority

Stop piecing together fragmented workflows. See how the Vixens Toolbox stacks up against the industry standard.

QEM Mesh Decimation

Vixens Toolbox

Real Quadric Error Metric edge-collapse decimation (Garland-Heckbert), the same class of algorithm as Blender's Decimate modifier. Drives each heavy mesh to a triangle target while preventing face flips and preserving UV/normal seams, submesh boundaries, blendshapes, and bone weights, non-destructively inside Unity.

Industry Standard

Requires manual decimation in external software (Blender) or expensive paid Unity plugins. High risk of permanently breaking visemes and facial animations during round-trip export.

Native Avatar Shader

VixenWear Latex Ultra (sold separately)

A native, dual-lobe GGX PBR surface shader engineered in-house for synthetic materials: clearcoat, thin-film, anisotropic latex-stretch specular, wet / drip and melting-goo, four Scene-placed decals, and live reactions to AudioLink, LTCGI, VRC Light Volumes and VRSL. Ships with a tessellation-free SPS twin for VRCFury. This is a standalone product, not part of the free toolbox package.

Poiyomi / lilToon

Mature, general-purpose toon / PBR shaders, and the right default for most avatars. They are not specialised for latex or synthetic surfaces, so matching the wet, dual-lobe, world-reactive look means stacking third-party modules and hand-tuning.

Protected Geometry

Vixens Toolbox

Automatically shields delicate geometry from the decimation grid. Material slots reading as eye, visor, lens, blush, face, mouth, teeth, pupil or iris are locked out, and on a Humanoid rig the vertices weighted to the left and right Hand bones are locked too, so fingers survive an aggressive pass.

Industry Standard

Uses blind decimation algorithms that routinely melt facial geometry. Requires tedious manual weight painting or masking in external software to protect detail.

Deep VRAM Material Spidering

Vixens Toolbox

Walks Unity's dependency graph through Animator controllers, VRCFury components and materials to catch textures buried in outfit toggles and animation clips, then measures the real GPU footprint rather than estimating it.

Industry Standard

Basic surface-level scanning. Only reads active Renderer materials attached to the base prefab, severely underreporting actual VRAM overhead on complex avatars.

Interactive Physics Executioner

Vixens Toolbox

UI Toolkit dynamically maps every physics component sorted by hierarchy depth. Surgically cull expendable leaf-node physics while visually protecting roots.

Industry Standard

Relies on blunt, invisible auto-cull algorithms or requires users to manually hunt down and blindly delete GameObjects across a massive, unorganized hierarchy.

Omni-System World Diagnostics

Vixens Toolbox

Powered by the Vixen World Engine spider - a unified auditor running around 137 checks across nine ecosystems (ProTV, TXL, VizVid, IwaSync3, AudioLink, LTCGI, Rinvo, VRC Light Volumes, VRSL) plus native video players and Udon persistence, in a single sweep. Detects GSV conflicts, Realtime GI blowouts, canvas rebuild cascades, and continuous sync storms, most with a one-click fix. Includes a raw UASM bytecode scraper capable of catching lethal PlayerData.Set rate‑limit bombs hidden inside Update loops long before they detonate in a live instance.

Industry Standard

Requires manual, piecemeal inspection of Udon behaviours, ProTV settings, TXL triggers, and IwaSync3 internals, each with its own inspector and its own conventions. Developers typically fall back on trial‑and‑error in live instances to diagnose bandwidth sinks, sync storms, or network rate‑limit failures, often discovering issues only after users report crashes or desyncs.

Context-Aware Dual-SDK Arch

Vixens Toolbox

Airtight compiler sandboxing between Avatars and Worlds. Dynamically hot-swaps UI and mathematically strips incompatible scripts based on active project SDKs.

Industry Standard

Prone to catastrophic missing Udon/AvatarDescriptor reference bricks when migrating tools between fundamentally different VRChat project infrastructures.

Core Workflow & Focus

Vixens Toolbox Ecosystem

All-in-one workflow: Integrates Quest conversion, PhysBones, animations, presets, optimization, and badges. Best for creators wanting a streamlined, pro pipeline with a strong Quest focus.

Fragmented Standards

Each tool is excellent within its own lane, and you assemble the pipeline yourself. VRCFury handles everyday avatar building (non-destructive toggles and clothing). VRCQuestTools is a deep platform-targeting suite built on NDMF. Pumkin's handles utilities, stats and quick setups.

Quest Conversion & Textures

Built-In Engine

Gamma-correct Magick.NET/Lanczos resampling with an adaptive sharpen pass to recover micro-contrast before ASTC compression. Clones the avatar into an isolated workspace, hard-locks components mobile will not accept, remaps materials to your chosen mobile shader, and maps every VRChat limit against your target performance rank in one reviewable panel.

VRCQuestTools / d4rk

Both are strong, and both are a different shape. VRCQuestTools bakes shader properties down per-shader (dedicated Poiyomi and lilToon generators) and compresses via astcenc, non-destructively through NDMF. d4rk runs on upload and specialises in merging meshes, materials and blend shapes. Neither presents the whole mobile budget as one auditable surface before you convert.

Physics Setup

PhysBone Blueprints

Master Blueprints allow for the automated extraction and injection of entire PhysBone layouts across different avatar versions seamlessly.

VRCFury / Others

VRCFury provides strong built-in support for adding/editing PhysBones non-destructively, but cannot save a whole PhysBone setup and re-apply it to another avatar.

Optimization Pipeline

Reviewable Culling

One scan reports unused empty objects, disabled components, oversized bounds, unused end bones and heavy meshes as a ranked task list. Nothing runs until you tick it. Textures still sitting on Uncompressed are raised first, above the resize panel, because moving to high-quality block compression cuts VRAM 4x without touching a single pixel of resolution.

d4rk / Thry's

d4rk hooks the upload itself and excels at merging meshes, materials and blend shapes, with its own texture quality analysis. Thry's shader optimizer bakes material properties into shader constants to strip dynamic branches. Both are deep in their own domain; neither audits the avatar as a whole.

Animation & Utilities

Advanced Editor Tools

Animation Workbench Pro and the Pipeline Preset Manager load in every project; world projects also get Live Surface Snapping and Precision Click-to-Place for scene layout. Curve authoring, material and component binding, and project-wide importer standards, without leaving the editor.

VRCFury / Pumkin's

VRCFury handles FX layer merging and menu management. Pumkin's covers bounding boxes and quick setups. Curve work still falls back to Unity's own Animation window, and Gesture Manager is a separate install.

UX & Accessibility

UI Toolkit Hub

Modern Vixen Hub with tabs, Markdown, and dynamic actions. Zero legacy redraw lag. Highly accessible for beginners as centralization reduces piecing things together.

Dated / Modular

Clean but relies on separate UIs per tool. Simple editor windows that can feel dated. Medium beginner difficulty due to requiring combining tools.

Translation & Safety

Isolated Sandboxes

Heavy emphasis on error-proofing. Uses isolated prefab sandboxes. PC to Mobile shader mapping handles 3rd party overrides natively.

Basic Replacements

Basic material replacement and standard overrides. Safety relies heavily on on-upload duplication or varies by destructive workflows depending on the tool.

Convention Badge Studio

Vixen Badge Studio

Procedural convention badge generation. Auto-detects Furality SDKs, targets Poiyomi / lilToon / VRChat Mobile / legacy badge shaders, generates directory structures, materials and emissive maps, and snaps text and neon accents to each year's badge mesh via a live Scene-view UV mapper.

Manual Per-Event Setup

No dedicated tool. Attendees hand-place text in an image editor, guess at UV coordinates on curved badge meshes, and rebuild materials and emission maps for every convention, every year.

Auto-Fit Culling Bounds

Posed-Bounds Auto-Fit

Fits each renderer's culling box to Unity's own posed bounds in the correct root-bone space, then adds a small margin for animation range. Works on any rig, humanoid or not, and handles Blender import scales that trip up hand-built matrices. Runs as one reviewable task inside the same scan that reports the rest of the avatar.

Pumkin's / Manual

Pumkin's samples a set of pose clips and unions the result, which captures animation range well, but requires a Humanoid rig. Hand-rolled approaches usually settle for one blunt box or a flat multiplier: over-sized wastes culling budget, under-sized makes meshes vanish the moment a viewer gets close.
