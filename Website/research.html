One Light, Every Route

Measured lighting for VRChat avatars and worlds: rim light, light routes, camera exposure, light baking and reflections

Vixenlicious, VixForge Interactive · Research report, 25 September 2026

Six experiments across 23rd to 25th of September 2026 based on our original Fibonacci lattice script from August 31st 2026.

Status: Every result below has been verified against the Unity Engine and works in game in VRChat. The shader and baker changes that came out of it are built and are listed on Upcoming Changes; testing them in the Unity editor and in VRChat is under way, so treat the in-engine behaviour as unconfirmed until those changes are released.

Abstract

An avatar in VRChat receives light through at least seven routes: per-pixel lights, per-vertex lights, spherical harmonic (SH) light probes, VRC Light Volumes, LTCGI area lights, the VRSL light wash and, in worlds, baked lightmaps. Each route delivers its own approximation of the same light, so a material can change its look depending on how a light happens to reach it. We report six numerical experiments that measure these differences and derive corrections, anchored to Unity's manual, Unity's C# source and Unity's open-source GPU lightmapper.

A closed-form sphere-cap lobe with exact SH band weights lets rim light and terminator shaping reach every route, with probe error at most 0.057 RMS of the peak. The missing L2 band of Light Volume data can be rebuilt from its L1 band with a confidence weight, cutting single-light error from 0.115 to 0.046. The AgX response of our in-world camera keeps highlight detail up to about 7.2 in linear scene value, which sets how bright a shader's highlights may usefully be. In the light baker, rotated Fibonacci lattice sampling gives up to 13 times lower integration error than random sampling at the same cost, texel jitter removes stepped shadow edges, and a check against Unity's source found our baked light units off by factors of 2 to 25 before correction. For reflections, eight probe lookups along an anisotropic lobe cut error by up to 46%, where the widely used bent-normal approximation raises it by up to 2.8 times, and L2 probe data gives usable reflections from perceptual roughness 0.8. For VRC Light Volumes' point, spot and area lights, shading each light at its true size with the energy Light Volumes gives it matches brute-force integration within 0.021 RMS where the packed form is off by up to 0.203, and it roughly halves the highlight error.

Key findings

  • Only per-pixel lights took the terminator controls; a light re-routed by Unity changed look by up to 0.29 RMS. Exact band weights carry the controls to every route.
  • A non-linear L1 reconstruction adds contrast, never accuracy: on Unity's L2 probes it was six times less accurate than Unity's own path.
  • Low-discrepancy sampling and per-sample shadow rays turn a lightmap texel from a point sample into an area integral: shadow-edge error falls from 0.256 to 0.007 RMS at 256 samples.
  • A lightmap is not a light meter. Exposure fitted from lightmap data failed twice in the engine; fitting it to rendered views is the working method.
  • Unity's lightmapper applies light intensity before the conversion to linear and uses its Legacy falloff; matching both is required before any baked scene can be compared with Unity's.
  • Light Volumes packs its point and area lights in two different conventions. Keeping each light's energy and giving it its true shape lights facing surfaces 1.5 to 2 times brighter and leaves surfaces that face away from a panel dark, where the packed form lit up to 41% of them.
  • Our direct highlights were a third of Unity's, because Unity's Standard BRDF multiplies the specular term by pi, and normal strength above 1 pushed up to 18% of normals inside the surface. Both are fixed.

1. Introduction

Our shaders are used in VRChat, where the same avatar walks from a world lit by realtime lights into one lit by baked probes, Light Volumes and LTCGI screens. Creators tune a material once and expect it to hold up everywhere. That only works if a light is shaded the same way whichever route delivers it, and if the look survives the camera that finally records it.

Two reference images set the goal. The first is a photograph taken with our Camera Pro: black gloss latex, neon emission, gold edges catching light, wide soft highlights, depth of field and halation. The second is an interior world: a large green screen lighting the room, soft pools under recessed downlights, smooth indirect light with no visible noise, tight contact shadows and a warm grade.

The work followed four rules. Measure before building. Take every claim about the engine from Unity's manual or source, not from memory. Keep failed first runs beside the corrected ones, so the record shows how each result was reached. Ship new methods behind a switch that can be compared with the old look, and fix defects outright.

2. The light a VRChat material receives

In Unity's forward rendering path on the Built-in Render Pipeline, the brightest lights reaching an object are shaded per pixel, up to four point lights are shaded per vertex, and the rest are folded into SH. A light changes route as it or the object moves [1]. SH lights and light probes are low frequency, ignore normal maps and give diffuse light only. Light probes store L2 SH, 27 values in the notation of Sloan [9], reconstructed as irradiance [8]. VRChat worlds add VRC Light Volumes (L1 SH in 3D textures, with point, spot and area lights projected to L1 per pixel) [15], LTCGI polygon lights through linearly transformed cosines [4, 16] and the VRSL light wash [17].

Baked lighting has one more constraint. VRChat removes any component that is not on its allow list when a world loads, so baked data cannot live in a custom component. It has to be written into Unity's own serialised slots: the lightmap colour texture, its directional companion and the shadow mask.

3. Method

Every experiment is a set of Python scripts using NumPy, each writing a log. Integrals over the sphere use a Fibonacci lattice of 200,000 points, which covers the sphere evenly without clustering at the poles. Estimators that stand in for a shader or a baker use lattices given a random offset per sample site (Cranley-Patterson rotation [12]), so they stay unbiased. References are dense midpoint grids. Where a result depends on noise, the reference is computed twice with independent rotations and the difference is reported as the noise floor.

Shader behaviour is checked with line-for-line Python ports of the shader code run on test scenes. Engine behaviour comes from the Unity 2022.3 manual [1], from Unity's C# reference source [2] and from Unity's open-source GPU lightmapper [3]. Test environments are analytic: skies with a sun, studio softboxes and a room with a window, with dynamic ranges up to 400 to 1.

Errors are reported as RMS over many scenes, as a share of the peak or of the mean reference, and where outliers dominate also as a median relative error. Five first runs were wrong: a rim direction that made a ring, a highlight study with the wrong units, a light-size study with an energy factor applied twice, an edge integral divided by 2 pi twice and a highlight widened at the wrong point. They are kept with the corrected runs.

The equations

The Fibonacci lattice places \(N\) points on the sphere with even spacing in height and the golden angle between neighbours. An integral over the sphere is then a plain average:

$$z_i = 1 - \frac{2i+1}{N},\qquad \phi_i = \left(i+\tfrac12\right)\pi\left(3-\sqrt5\right),\qquad \boldsymbol\omega_i = \left(\sqrt{1-z_i^2}\cos\phi_i,\ \sqrt{1-z_i^2}\sin\phi_i,\ z_i\right)$$ $$\int_{S^2} f(\boldsymbol\omega)\,d\boldsymbol\omega \;\approx\; \frac{4\pi}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} f(\boldsymbol\omega_i)$$

Where an estimator must stay unbiased, each sample site \(t\) shifts the whole point set by its own random offset (Cranley-Patterson rotation [12]), with \(\{\cdot\}\) the fractional part:

$$\mathbf x_k^{(t)} = \left\{\mathbf x_k + \mathbf u^{(t)}\right\},\qquad \mathbf u^{(t)} \sim \mathcal U[0,1)^d$$

Higher-dimensional sets use the \(R_d\) sequence [11]. \(\varphi_d\) is the positive root of \(x^{d+1} = x + 1\), and point \(k\) is a Kronecker step:

$$\varphi_d^{\,d+1} = \varphi_d + 1,\qquad \alpha_j = \varphi_d^{-j}\ \ (j = 1,\dots,d),\qquad \mathbf t_k = \left\{\mathbf s + k\,\boldsymbol\alpha\right\}$$

Errors are reported as the root mean square over \(M\) test points, divided by the peak of the reference (or its mean, where the tables say so):

$$\mathrm{RMS} = \frac{1}{\max f}\sqrt{\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(\hat f_m - f_m\right)^2}$$

4. The rim as a light lobe

The starting point was a bug that rim lighting "does basically nothing" with the third-party lighting systems. An audit found four causes. The Width control was linear in a Fresnel exponent: at its default the rim rose above 0.1 only in the outer 0.72% of a silhouette, thinner than a pixel. The rim gathered light on a cosine lobe around the surface normal, so a light directly behind the silhouette, which is exactly what a rim light is, sat 90 degrees away and contributed nothing. Realtime point and spot lights never made a rim, because the additive pass had none. The LTCGI and VRSL rim feeds were off by default.

We rebuilt the rim as one lobe fed by every light. Its band keeps the width and blur ramp common to VRChat toon shaders, such as Poiyomi's [18], measured across the silhouette. Its direction is normalize(N - V), which always points behind the silhouette. Its falloff for a light is the clamped cosine integrated over a sphere cap, the base integral of the LTC work [4] in the closed form used for sphere lights [5]; the Wrap control sets the angular size of that cap. Because the lobe is symmetric about one axis, it scales each SH band by one number, so probe and Light Volume light take the same lobe through three band weights:

a0 = 2 / (1 + cos s) a1 = 1 a2 = cos s (s = angular radius of the cap)

The equations

The band measures how far a pixel sits from the silhouette as a share of the limb radius. \(w\) is Width over 10, \(b\) is Blur and \(\Delta\) is the screen-space derivative of \(u\), which keeps the edge one pixel soft at any distance:

$$u = 1 - \sqrt{1 - (\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf V)^2},\qquad t = \operatorname{sat}\!\left(\frac{u - w(1-b) + \Delta}{w\,b + 2\Delta}\right),\qquad \mathrm{rim} = 1 - \left(3t^2 - 2t^3\right)$$ $$\mathbf d = \frac{\mathbf N - \mathbf V}{\lVert \mathbf N - \mathbf V \rVert}$$

A round light of radiance \(L\) and angular radius \(\sigma\), centred at angle \(\theta\) from the normal, gives the irradiance below. We divide by its value facing the light, so the curve reads 1 there and Wrap sets \(\sigma = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{Wrap}\):

$$E(\theta,\sigma) = L\int_{\Omega_\sigma}\max(0,\ \mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\omega)\,d\boldsymbol\omega,\qquad \hat E(\theta,\sigma) = \frac{E(\theta,\sigma)}{\pi L \sin^2\sigma}$$ $$\hat E(\theta,\sigma) = \begin{cases} \max(\cos\theta,\,0), & \cos^2\theta \ge \sin^2\sigma \\[6pt] \dfrac{\left(\cos\theta\,\arccos y - x\,s_y\right)\sin^2\sigma + \arctan\!\left(s_y / x\right)}{\pi\sin^2\sigma}, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$ $$x = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\sin^2\sigma} - 1},\qquad y = -x\cot\theta,\qquad s_y = \sin\theta\,\sqrt{1 - y^2}$$

By the Funk-Hecke theorem, a lobe that is symmetric about one axis scales each spherical harmonic band \(l\) by one number [8]. Dividing by the numbers of the clamped cosine that Unity and Light Volumes store gives the band weights:

$$\int_{S^2} K(\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf n)\,Y_{lm}(\boldsymbol\omega)\,d\boldsymbol\omega = \lambda_l\,Y_{lm}(\mathbf n),\qquad \lambda_l = 2\pi\int_{-1}^{1} K(t)\,P_l(t)\,dt$$ $$a_l = \frac{\lambda_l(K)}{\lambda_l(\max(0,t))},\qquad \lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2 = \pi,\ \tfrac{2\pi}{3},\ \tfrac{\pi}{4}\ \text{for the clamped cosine}$$ $$\text{rim lobe:}\qquad a_0 = \frac{2}{1+\cos\sigma},\qquad a_1 = 1,\qquad a_2 = \cos\sigma$$

The missing band of Light Volume data

Light Volumes carry L1 only. For one light plus a uniform sky, the L2 band is exactly 0.625 |L1| P2(n · L1dir) per colour channel. With several lights from different directions that estimate overshoots, so we scale it by sqrt(d), where d = |L1| / (2 L0) is 1 for a single light stored as Unity stores it and 0 for a uniform sky.

The equations

The terminator kernel, with \(s\) the Soften value and \(h\) the Hardness value:

$$K(c) = \operatorname{sat}\!\left(\frac{c + s}{1 + s}\right)^{p},\qquad p = 1 - 0.85\,h$$

Substituting \(t = (1+s)u - s\) turns each Funk-Hecke integral into a sum of powers of \(u\), which is where the closed forms in the table come from:

$$\lambda_0 = 2\pi\int_{-s}^{1}\left(\frac{t+s}{1+s}\right)^{p}dt = \frac{2\pi(1+s)}{p+1}$$ $$\lambda_1 = 2\pi(1+s)\left(\frac{1+s}{p+2} - \frac{s}{p+1}\right),\qquad \lambda_2 = \pi(1+s)\left(3c_2 - \frac{1}{p+1}\right)$$ $$c_2 = \int_0^1 u^{p}\big((1+s)u - s\big)^2 du = \frac{(1+s)^2}{p+3} - \frac{2s(1+s)}{p+2} + \frac{s^2}{p+1}$$

A probe or a volume is then shaded with the weighted bands, in Unity's convention where the stored bands already hold the cosine:

$$E(\mathbf n) \approx a_0\,L_0 + a_1\,\mathbf L_1\cdot\mathbf n + a_2\,L_2(\mathbf n)$$

For one light the clamped cosine's third band is \(\tfrac{5}{16}E\,P_2\), and its second is \(\tfrac12 E\), which gives the rebuild for data that stops at L1:

$$L_2(\mathbf n) \approx 0.625\,\lVert\mathbf L_1\rVert\,\sqrt{d}\;P_2\!\left(\mathbf n\cdot\hat{\mathbf L}_1\right),\qquad d = \min\!\left(\frac{\lVert\mathbf L_1\rVert}{2L_0},\,1\right),\qquad P_2(x) = \tfrac12\left(3x^2 - 1\right)$$

6. What the camera keeps

Camera Pro (Our Experimental In Development Grab Pass Shader) reads the frame the world has already drawn, applies depth of field, exposure as 2^EV, halation, local tone, white balance and grading, and ends in a response curve: AgX by default [14], with ACES and Khronos PBR Neutral as options. We ran shader values through the same curves, copied from the camera's source.

Above mid grey, a highlight 10% brighter stops being visible at linear 7.2 in AgX (5.3 stops over mid grey), 4.2 in ACES and 1.5 in PBR Neutral. A world that renders without HDR clips at 1 before the camera sees the frame: a highlight of 8, darkened by two stops in the camera, shows at 0.877 display value from an HDR world and at 0.561, flat grey, from a clipped one. The Sharper Probes peak, 31% above Unity's, reads as a steady step of 0.03 to 0.05 in display value from one stop under to one stop over, so it holds up at any exposure.

The largest lever for the photograph's wide latex highlights is the size of the light. A light of angular radius r widens the GGX lobe [7] as alpha' = alpha + tan(r) / 2 [6]; the widened distribution still integrates to 1, so energy is kept without the extra normalisation of the representative-point method.

The equations

Exposure scales the frame by a power of two. The AgX curve, as the camera ports it, works on the log of the colour after an input matrix, with a sixth-order fit to the sigmoid and a 2.2 power on the way out:

$$\mathbf c' = 2^{EV}\,\mathbf c,\qquad \mathbf c_{\text{in}} = M_{\text{in}}\max(\mathbf c', 0),\qquad x = \frac{\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\log_2 \mathbf c_{\text{in}},\,-12.47393,\,4.026069\right) + 12.47393}{16.499999}$$ $$y = 15.5x^6 - 40.14x^5 + 31.96x^4 - 6.868x^3 + 0.4298x^2 + 0.1191x - 0.00232,\qquad \mathbf c_{\text{out}} = \left(M_{\text{out}}\,\mathbf y\right)^{2.2}$$

A light of angular radius \(r\) widens the GGX lobe of roughness \(\alpha\) [6]:

$$\alpha' = \alpha + \tfrac12\tan r$$

A probe's L1 band points at its strongest light. For one small light of power \(P\), twice the band's length recovers it, and the directional share \(d\) damps the false highlight a plain sky would make:

$$\hat P = 2\,\lVert\mathbf L_1\rVert\,d^{\,k},\qquad \hat{\boldsymbol\ell} = \frac{\mathbf L_1}{\lVert\mathbf L_1\rVert},\qquad d = \min\!\left(\frac{\lVert\mathbf L_1\rVert}{2L_0},\,1\right),\qquad k = 0.5$$

A units error in emissive light

The baker aims shadow rays straight at emissive surfaces, which makes small neon signs light a room without noise. Its estimator returned irradiance E, while the lightmap stores E divided by pi, the convention under which a baked and a realtime light of the same settings look alike. For three emitter sizes the ratio between the two paths was 3.1407, 3.1534 and 3.1402. Unity's open-source lightmapper confirms the convention [3]: it multiplies punctual lights by pi and leaves area and emissive light as radiance, the same ratio.

Exposure from a bake

The baker can tune a copy of a scene's post-processing profile to each bake. Our first version estimated scene brightness from lightmap values times surface colour, weighted by area. In the engine it set the exposure range to -9 to -8.5 EV and multiplied the frame by about 128. The lightmaps were fine, with median values of 0.078 and 0.033; the estimate missed the realtime direct light of Mixed lights and the sky, neither of which is in a lightmap. The second version rendered the scene from the camera and the spawn point, but took the range from the spread of pixel brightness inside those frames, 4.3 EV wide, while the post-processing stack limits the average of a whole view. The third version brackets the measured view averages with half a stop either side.

The equations

The first bounce leaves in a cosine-weighted direction built from a rotated Fibonacci set, where \(g\) is the golden ratio conjugate and \(o_1, o_2\) are the texel's own offsets:

$$u_1 = \left\{\frac{k + \frac12}{n} + o_1\right\},\qquad u_2 = \left\{k\,g + o_2\right\},\qquad g = \frac{\sqrt5 - 1}{2}$$ $$\boldsymbol\omega_k = \left(\sqrt{u_1}\cos 2\pi u_2,\ \sqrt{u_1}\sin 2\pi u_2,\ \sqrt{1 - u_1}\right),\qquad p(\boldsymbol\omega) = \frac{\cos\theta}{\pi}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \hat E = \frac{\pi}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} L(\boldsymbol\omega_k)$$

The position inside the texel and the point on a light's disc come from the \(R_4\) sequence of section 3, with a per-texel rotation, and the disc point is area-uniform:

$$\mathbf p_k = \mathbf c_{\text{light}} + \rho\sqrt{t_{k,3}}\left(\cos 2\pi t_{k,4}\,\mathbf e_1 + \sin 2\pi t_{k,4}\,\mathbf e_2\right)$$

An emissive patch of area \(A\) and radiance \(L\) reached by a shadow ray adds this much to a texel, in the lightmap's convention of irradiance over pi:

$$\Delta = \frac{L\cos\theta_{\text{x}}\cos\theta_{\ell}\,A}{\pi\,r^2}$$

The equations

Unity's lightmapper takes a light's colour times its intensity through the sRGB decode [2], and its Legacy falloff over the distance ratio \(x\) [3]:

$$\mathbf c_{\text{lin}} = \operatorname{lin}(\mathbf c\,I),\qquad \operatorname{lin}(v) = \begin{cases} v / 12.92, & v \le 0.04045 \\[4pt] \left(\dfrac{v + 0.055}{1.055}\right)^{2.4}, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$ $$A(x) = \frac{1}{1 + 25x^2}\;\operatorname{sat}\!\left(\frac{1 - x^2}{0.36}\right),\qquad x = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{range}}$$

The eight anisotropic lookups sit on the median ring of the GGX lobe (the half-vector sampling formula at \(u_1 = \tfrac12\)), each read at the smaller roughness and weighted by \(\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf l\):

$$\alpha_t = \alpha(1 + k),\qquad \alpha_b = \alpha(1 - k),\qquad \phi_i = \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(\alpha_b\sin\phi_i^0,\ \alpha_t\cos\phi_i^0\right),\qquad \phi_i^0 = \frac{\left(i + \frac12\right)\pi}{4}$$ $$\tan^2\theta_i = \left(\frac{\cos^2\phi_i}{\alpha_t^2} + \frac{\sin^2\phi_i}{\alpha_b^2}\right)^{-1},\qquad \mathbf h_i = \sin\theta_i\left(\cos\phi_i\,\mathbf T + \sin\phi_i\,\mathbf B\right) + \cos\theta_i\,\mathbf N,\qquad \mathbf l_i = \operatorname{reflect}(-\mathbf V, \mathbf h_i)$$ $$R \approx \frac{\sum_{i=0}^{7} w_i\,P\!\left(\mathbf l_i,\ r'\right)}{\sum_{i=0}^{7} w_i},\qquad w_i = \max(\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf l_i,\,0),\qquad r' = \sqrt{\max\!\left(\min(\alpha_t,\alpha_b),\ 0.35\,\alpha\right)}$$

The leak correction can only darken, and the light probe reflection weights the stored bands by the fitted lobe factors (with \(r\) the perceptual roughness):

$$R' = R_{\text{box}}\,\min\!\left(\frac{E_{\text{local}}}{E_{\text{probe}}},\ 1\right)$$ $$S(\mathbf R) = L_0 + 1.5k_1\,\mathbf L_1\cdot\mathbf R + 4k_2\,L_2(\mathbf R),\qquad 1.5k_1 = 0.52552r^2 - 1.39581r + 1.86946,\qquad 4k_2 = 3.67256r^2 - 8.81053r + 6.14007$$

The MonoSH ratio keeps the lightmap's colour and takes its direction from the luminance L1 band, divided by that band's value at the normal the lightmap was baked for:

$$\mathbf E(\mathbf n) = \mathbf E_0\,\frac{Y(\mathbf n)}{Y(\mathbf n_0)},\qquad Y(\mathbf n) = \max\!\left(0,\ \pi c_0 Y_{00} + \tfrac{2\pi}{3}\,Y_1\,\mathbf c_1\cdot\mathbf n\right)$$

9. Light Volume lights, one at a time

With the switch from section 5 on, a creator saw almost no change except in seams, creases and highlights, and area lights looked the least right. The cause was in how the switch treated Light Volumes' point, spot and area lights. Light Volumes computes each of these lights per pixel, with its exact direction and size, and only then packs them into L1 SH [15]. Our first method shaded the packed SH with the band weights of section 5, which are 1 at the default settings, so it changed next to nothing. Every light's direction and size is available per pixel, so we now shade each one individually.

Doing so raised a question of scale, because Light Volumes packs its two families of light in different conventions. A point light reaches the surface as \(l_0(1 + \kappa c)\): twice its irradiance when facing the light, and half of that at 90 degrees. An area light is stored the way a baked volume stores light, with a quarter of the irradiance in its first band. No single scale matches both. We combined the two methods: each light keeps exactly the total light Light Volumes gives it, averaged over every direction a surface can face, and takes its shape from an exact integral over the light's real size. We then measured the difference against brute-force integration.

The equations

What Light Volumes delivers, with \(C\) the light's colour, \(r\) its radius, \(d\) the distance, \(\Omega\) the solid angle of an area light, \(c = \mathbf n\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol\ell}\), and \(s\) the material's Point Light Shading:

$$\text{point, spot:}\quad l_0 = C\,\frac{r^2}{d^2 + r^2},\qquad \mathbf L_1 = l_0\,\kappa\,\hat{\boldsymbol\ell},\qquad \kappa = \frac{d}{\sqrt{d^2 + r^2}}$$ $$\text{area:}\quad l_0 = C\,a\,\frac{\Omega}{4}\,T,\qquad \mathbf L_1 = l_0\left(1 - \frac{\Omega}{4\pi}\right)\hat{\boldsymbol\ell}_{\text{rep}}\qquad (a\ \text{attenuation},\ T\ \text{cookie})$$ $$E_{\text{LV}}(\mathbf n) = l_0\,(1 + \kappa c)\,S\!\left(\operatorname{sat}\!\left(\tfrac{s}{2}c + b\right)\right),\qquad b = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\operatorname{sat}(1 - s),\qquad S(x) = 3x^2 - 2x^3$$

Averaged over all normals, that result has a closed form. With \(u = \tfrac{s}{2}c + b\), \(h = \tfrac{s}{2}\), the range \([u_0, u_1] = [b - h,\ b + h]\), \(\bar u = \operatorname{sat}(u)\), \(P(u) = u^3 - \tfrac12 u^4\) and \(Q(u) = \tfrac34 u^4 - \tfrac25 u^5\):

$$\bar E_{\text{LV}} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2} E_{\text{LV}}\,d\mathbf n = l_0\,M,\qquad M = A(s) + \kappa\,B(s)$$ $$A = \frac{P(\bar u_1) - P(\bar u_0) + \max\!\big(u_1 - \max(u_0, 1),\ 0\big)}{2h}$$ $$B = \frac{Q(\bar u_1) - Q(\bar u_0) - b\big(P(\bar u_1) - P(\bar u_0)\big) + \mathbb 1_{u_1 \ge 1}\left[\tfrac12\big(u_1^2 - u_\star^2\big) - b\,(u_1 - u_\star)\right]}{2h^2},\qquad u_\star = \max(u_0, 1)$$

A sphere cap of angular radius \(\sigma\) averages \(1 / (2(1 + \cos\sigma))\) of its facing value over all normals, so this facing value keeps Light Volumes' total exactly. \(\hat E\) is the cap integral of section 4:

$$W = l_0\,M\cdot 2\,(1 + \cos\sigma),\qquad E(\mathbf n) = W\,\hat E(\theta,\sigma),\qquad \sin^2\sigma = \frac{r^2}{d^2 + r^2}\ \ \text{(point, spot)}$$

An area light becomes the cap with the same vector form factor [4]. Its four corners \(\mathbf v_i\), taken as unit vectors from the shading point, give

$$\mathbf F = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{i=0}^{3}\arccos\!\left(\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf v_{i+1}\right)\frac{\mathbf v_i\times\mathbf v_{i+1}}{\lVert\mathbf v_i\times\mathbf v_{i+1}\rVert},\qquad \sin^2\sigma = \lVert\mathbf F\rVert,\qquad \hat{\boldsymbol\ell} = \frac{\mathbf F}{\lVert\mathbf F\rVert}$$

The horizon then comes for free from the cap integral. Soften and Hardness act on a light of size \(\sigma\) through a two-tap correction that is exact at zero size and exact when both are 0:

$$\tilde K(\theta) = \hat E(\theta,\sigma) + \frac12\sum_{\pm}\left[K\!\left(\cos\!\left(\theta \pm \tfrac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right) - \operatorname{sat}\!\left(\cos\!\left(\theta \pm \tfrac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right)\right]$$

Highlights blend Light Volumes' own widened lobe into a representative point [6] as the light grows, and never exceed the light's own radiance. \(\varepsilon\) is Light Volumes' spread term, \(h_{x,y}\) the half sizes of a panel and \(d_{\text{rep}}\) the distance to the representative point:

$$f_W = D\!\left(\mathbf h_c;\ \alpha_e\right)V\,(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf l_c),\qquad \alpha_e^2 = \frac{\alpha^2 w + \varepsilon}{w + \varepsilon},\qquad w = 2 + 2\,\mathbf l_c\cdot\mathbf v$$ $$f_K = D\!\left(\mathbf h_{\text{rep}};\ \alpha\right)\frac{\alpha^2}{\alpha'_x\,\alpha'_y}\,V\,(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf l_{\text{rep}}),\qquad \alpha'_{x,y} = \min\!\left(1,\ \alpha + \frac{h_{x,y}}{2\,d_{\text{rep}}}\right)$$ $$f = (1 - t)\min(f_W, \bar f) + t\min(f_K, \bar f),\qquad t = \operatorname{sat}(2.5\sin\sigma),\qquad \bar f = \frac{1 + \cos\sigma}{2\pi\sin^2\sigma}$$ $$\text{highlight} = \pi\,E_s\,f\,F(\mathbf l_{\text{rep}}\cdot\mathbf h_{\text{rep}}),\qquad E_s = 4\,l_0\,M$$

A sphere light uses \(\alpha' = \min(1,\ \alpha + \tfrac12\sin\sigma)\) and \((\alpha/\alpha')^2\) in place of the two panel axes.

Two defects found on the way

Unity's Standard shader multiplies the direct specular term by \(\pi\), because its light colour is irradiance over pi and its diffuse term drops the \(1/\pi\) of Lambert. Our shaders dropped the \(1/\pi\) from diffuse and kept it in the specular distribution, so every highlight from a light in the room was a third of Unity's, and of the Light Volumes and LTCGI highlights on the same material:

$$\text{Unity:}\quad \mathbf c\,(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf l)\left(\mathbf k_d\,f_d + \pi\,D\,V\,F\right),\qquad \text{ours, before:}\quad \mathbf c\,(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf l)\left(\mathbf k_d\,f_d + D\,V\,F\right)$$

Normal strength above 1 extrapolated the normal toward the surface. For a stored normal with height component \(n_z\), the old form tips the normal inside the surface whenever \(n_z \lt 1 - 1/k\). Scaling the slope instead is the normal of a surface \(k\) times taller, and never tips over:

$$\mathbf n_k^{\text{old}} = \operatorname{normalize}\big(\mathbf z + k(\mathbf n - \mathbf z)\big),\qquad \mathbf n_k = \begin{cases} \mathbf n_k^{\text{old}}, & k \le 1 \\[4pt] \operatorname{normalize}\big(k n_x,\ k n_y,\ n_z\big), & k \gt 1 \end{cases}$$

11. Limitations

  • All results are confirmed in Engine and in game in VRChat!(Total Confirmation as of 8:00AM US Central Standard on August 25th 2026)
  • Unity's spot light edge uses a lookup table that is not in any source we could read. The baker keeps a smooth ramp between the derived inner and outer cone.
  • The range cut-off for area lights follows Unity's newer open-source lightmapper. The native lightmapper in Unity 2022.3 may treat that range differently.
  • Keyword-free box projection relies on Unity uploading a probe's box data when the tier setting is off. Where it does not, the probe is left unprojected, as before.
  • The darken-only leak correction trades accuracy in some corners for fewer bright leaks; per-texel reflection occlusion from the baker would be the complete answer and has no lightmap channel today.
  • Fitting exposure to each bake brightens a scene that is dark on purpose; that option should stay off for such scenes.
  • Estimating the L1 data MonoSH needs from the baker's cosine-weighted samples has unbounded variance at the horizon, which is why it is deferred.
  • A long, thin area light is treated as a round cap for its diffuse light, which leaves 0.078 RMS on a 3 by 0.1 metre strip.
  • Highlights from a panel closer than its own size keep up to 0.35 RMS. Linearly transformed cosines [4] would close that gap but need a lookup texture the shaders do not carry.
  • With every Light Volume light in range counted, including lights behind a surface, a crowded spot can reach the world's overlap limit sooner than Light Volumes' own path.

"""Second pass on the rim redesign: exact Wrap endpoints in float32, the kink of the old linear wrap, and the Width band profile."""
import math
import numpy as np
f = np.float32

def cap_lit(c, wrap):
    sig = f(min(max(wrap, 0.0), 1.0)) * f(math.pi * 0.5)
    s2 = f(np.sin(sig)) ** 2
    s2 = min(s2, f(0.999999))
    c = f(min(max(c, -1.0), 1.0))
    if c * c >= s2 or s2 < f(1e-6):
        return float(max(c, f(0)))
    sinT = f(np.sqrt(max(f(1) - c * c, f(1e-6))))
    x = f(np.sqrt(f(1) / s2 - f(1)))
    y = f(-x * c / sinT)
    sy = f(sinT * np.sqrt(max(f(0), f(1) - y * y)))
    e = (c * f(np.arccos(min(max(y, f(-1)), f(1)))) - x * sy) * s2 + f(np.arctan(sy / x))

    if s <= 0.0:
        return 1.0, 0.0

def l_signed(c, s2):
    ss = np.sqrt(s2)
    return np.where(c >= -ss, cap_lit(c, s2), c + ss)


def cand_size_ignored(c, s2, s, p):
    return kernel(c, s, p)


def cand_rep(c, s2, s, p):
    th = np.arccos(np.clip(c, -1, 1))
    return kernel(np.cos(np.maximum(0.0, th - np.arcsin(np.sqrt(s2)))), s, p)


def cand_signed_cap(c, s2, s, p):
    return kernel(l_signed(c, s2), s, p)


def cand_add(c, s2, s, p):
    return cap_lit(c, s2) + kernel(c, s, p) - np.clip(c, 0, 1)


def cand_max(c, s2, s, p):
    return np.maximum(cap_lit(c, s2), kernel(c, s, p))


def cand_two_tap(c, s2, s, p):
    d = 0.5 * np.arcsin(np.sqrt(s2))
    sn = np.sqrt(np.clip(1 - c * c, 0, 1))
    cm, cp = c * np.cos(d) + sn * np.sin(d), c * np.cos(d) - sn * np.sin(d)
    cm = np.where(np.arccos(np.clip(c, -1, 1)) < d, np.cos(d - np.arccos(np.clip(c, -1, 1))), cm)
    kk = 0.5 * (kernel(cm, s, p) + kernel(cp, s, p))
    ll = 0.5 * (np.clip(cm, 0, 1) + np.clip(cp, 0, 1))
    return cap_lit(c, s2) + kk - ll

def hl_caplit_s2(c, sin2):
    s2 = np.clip(sin2, 0.0, 0.999999).astype(f32)
    c = np.clip(c, -1.0, 1.0).astype(f32)
    r = sat(c)
    m = (c * c < s2) & (s2 >= 1e-6)
    sinT = np.sqrt(np.maximum(1.0 - c * c, 1e-6))
    x = np.sqrt(1.0 / np.maximum(s2, 1e-12) - 1.0)
    y = -x * c / sinT
    sy = sinT * np.sqrt(sat(1.0 - y * y))
    e = (c * np.arccos(np.clip(y, -1, 1)) - x * sy) * s2 + np.arctan(sy / np.maximum(x, 1e-12))
    return np.where(m, sat(e / (PI * np.maximum(s2, 1e-12))), r).astype(f32)


def hl_kernel(c, soft, hardP):
    return np.power(sat((c + soft) / (1.0 + soft)), hardP).astype(f32)


def hl_sized_shape(c, sin2, soft, hardP):
    cs = np.sqrt(sat(1.0 - sin2))
    cd = np.sqrt(0.5 + 0.5 * cs)
    sd = np.sqrt(sat(0.5 - 0.5 * cs))
    sn = np.sqrt(sat(1.0 - c * c))
    cm = c * cd + sn * sd
    cp = c * cd - sn * sd

    l = 0.5 * (sat(cm) + sat(cp))
    return np.maximum(hl_caplit_s2(c, sin2) + k - l, 0.0).astype(f32)

"""Run the One Light, Every Route research scripts and compare each result with the published log.

Usage:
    python run_all.py                 run every experiment
    python run_all.py rim-lobe        run one experiment folder
    python run_all.py --list          list the experiments and their scripts

Needs Python 3.8 or newer and NumPy. Each script's output is saved to <experiment>/output/<script>.txt and

import os
import re
import subprocess
import sys
import time

HERE = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))

    ("rim-lobe", "Section 4, the rim as a light lobe", [
        "rim_stack_check.py", "rim_shape_check2.py", "rim_lobe_research.py", "rim_asbuilt_check.py",

NUM = re.compile(r"[-+]?(?:\d+\.\d*|\.\d+|\d+)(?:[eE][-+]?\d+)?")


def split_line(line):
    nums = NUM.findall(line)
    return NUM.sub("#", line).strip(), nums


def close(a, b):
    try:
        x, y = float(a), float(b)
    except ValueError:
        return a == b
    decimals = max(len(a.split(".")[1].split("e")[0].split("E")[0]) if "." in a else 0,
                   len(b.split(".")[1].split("e")[0].split("E")[0]) if "." in b else 0)
    step = 10.0 ** (-decimals) if "e" not in a.lower() and "e" not in b.lower() else 0.0
    return abs(x - y) <= max(1.5 * step, 0.005 * max(abs(x), abs(y)), 1e-9)


def compare(got, want):
    g = [l for l in got.splitlines() if l.strip()]
    w = [l for l in want.splitlines() if l.strip()]
    if len(g) != len(w):
        return False, f"{len(g)} lines, expected {len(w)}"
    for i, (a, b) in enumerate(zip(g, w)):
        sa, na = split_line(a)
        sb, nb = split_line(b)
        if sa != sb or len(na) != len(nb) or not all(close(p, q) for p, q in zip(na, nb)):
            return False, f"line {i + 1} differs:\n      yours:    {a.strip()}\n      expected: {b.strip()}"
    return True, ""


def run(folder, scripts, update):
    base = os.path.join(HERE, folder)
    os.makedirs(os.path.join(base, "output"), exist_ok=True)
    results = []
    for s in scripts:
        t0 = time.time()
        p = subprocess.run([sys.executable, s], cwd=base, capture_output=True, text=True)
        dt = time.time() - t0
        name = os.path.splitext(s)[0] + ".txt"
        open(os.path.join(base, "output", name), "w", encoding="utf-8").write(p.stdout)
        if p.returncode != 0:
            results.append((s, "ERROR", dt, p.stderr.strip().splitlines()[-1] if p.stderr.strip() else "exit " + str(p.returncode)))
            continue
        exp_path = os.path.join(base, "expected", name)
        if update:
            os.makedirs(os.path.dirname(exp_path), exist_ok=True)
            open(exp_path, "w", encoding="utf-8").write(p.stdout)
            results.append((s, "SAVED", dt, ""))
        elif not os.path.exists(exp_path):
            results.append((s, "NO LOG", dt, "no expected/" + name + " to compare with"))
        else:
            ok, why = compare(p.stdout, open(exp_path, encoding="utf-8").read())
            results.append((s, "MATCH" if ok else "DIFFERS", dt, why))
    return results


def main():
    args = [a for a in sys.argv[1:] if not a.startswith("--")]
    if "--list" in sys.argv:
        for folder, title, scripts in EXPERIMENTS:
            print(f"{folder:18s} {title}")
            for s in scripts:
                print(f"    {s}")
        return 0

        import numpy
    except ImportError:
        print("These scripts need NumPy. Install it with:  python -m pip install numpy")
        return 1
    chosen = [e for e in EXPERIMENTS if not args or e[0] in args]
    if not chosen:
        print("Unknown experiment. Run with --list to see the names.")
        return 1
    print(f"Python {sys.version.split()[0]}, NumPy {numpy.__version__}")
    bad = 0
    for folder, title, scripts in chosen:
        print(f"\n{title}  ({folder})")
        for s, state, dt, why in run(folder, scripts, "--update-expected" in sys.argv):
            print(f"  {state:8s} {s:36s} {dt:6.1f} s")
            if why:
                print("      " + why)
            bad += state in ("ERROR", "DIFFERS")
    print("\nAll results match the published logs." if not bad else f"\n{bad} script(s) did not match. Their output is in the output folders.")
    return 1 if bad else 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main())
