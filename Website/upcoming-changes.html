VixForge / World Surface 1.0.0 - Unreleased

A shader for the things a world is made of. Tile, glass, a bath tub, painted walls, polished floors, a sofa. It is the Toon shader with the wet and goo effects folded in from Latex Ultra and the fabric sheen from Clothing Pro, because a room needs all three.

Added

  • A shader built for worlds. Put it on any surface in your scene. Everything the avatar shaders do is here: the shading styles, five matcap layers, Car Paint, three glitter layers, five emission layers, decals, two outlines, and the whole set of special effects.
  • Wet Effects and Goo Effects. Water beads and runs down tile, soaks into stone and leaves a film on glass. Goo drips, clings, pools on the floor and crawls. They were built for latex on an avatar and they turn out to be exactly what a bathroom, a slime cave or a rain-soaked street wants.
  • Cloth and fibre, without geometry. The soft edge glow of silk, satin and velvet, and a painted-on fibre highlight for upholstery, carpet edges and moss on stone. Neither grows geometry, so neither costs you framerate.
  • LTCGI, and it belongs here. LTCGI's author discourages it on avatars and points them at Light Volumes instead. In a world it is the right tool: your emissive surfaces become real area lights, so a screen or a neon sign actually lights what is near it. Light Volumes, VRSL stage lighting and AudioLink are all here too, and they run together.
  • Every AudioLink effect comes across. The response engine, Chromatic Bloom, Note Swarm, Sonar, Spectrum Ribbon and the Cybernetic overlays. A club wall can read the music the same way an outfit can.
  • Lighting Check. Under DEBUG TOOLS. Paints the surface with the light actually reaching it, so you can see which system your world is lighting it with. Set it back to Off before you upload.
  • Your baked lighting is read and used. Mark your geometry Contribute GI, press Generate Lighting, and the shader reads the result. Directional lightmaps mean your normal maps still catch the baked light instead of going flat, and shadowmask lets baked and real-time shadows sit together. Subtractive mode works too, which is the one that suits a stylised or cel-shaded room.
  • It contributes to the bake as well. Its colour bounces into the room, and its glow lights what is near it. Under Baked Lighting, set Global Illumination to Baked to turn a material's glow into a light source, then use Emitted Light beside it to set how bright that is in the bake without touching how the material looks on screen. Neon and screens usually want more than 1. Double Sided Global Illumination is there for a sign or a panel that should light both ways.
  • Soft shadows and light cookies. The main directional light can cast soft shadows, point lights cast shadows now, and a light with a cookie projects it properly.
  • Smooth Filter, under Baked Lighting. Reads the baked lightmap with a smoother filter, so a low resolution bake stops looking blocky. On by default. It costs a few extra reads and no extra memory.
  • Highlight, under Baked Lighting. Baked light can now throw a specular highlight, so a polished floor or a wet tile is not flat under a bake. Set Directional Mode to Directional in your Lighting settings, or it has nothing to work from. Highlight Softness caps how sharp it gets, which hides the resolution of the lightmap.
  • Motion vectors are written now. A world that uses motion blur or temporal antialiasing has real per-object motion to work from. Nothing to set up.
  • Reflection Clamp, under LIGHTING. Eases down a blown highlight coming off reflection probes, Light Volumes, LTCGI or stage lighting, with a slider each so you can leave one source out of it. Its neighbour, Specular Highlight Clamp, does the same for your scene lights. Both are off by default.

Notes

  • The first lock after a bake takes a while. Supporting lightmaps, shadowmask and soft shadows means more shader variants to build. It is working, not stuck, so let it finish.
  • For carpets and rugs, use World Fur instead. Real Fur grows actual standing strands, and it lives in that shader. World Surface has the painted-on Surface Fur only.
VixForge / World Fur 1.0.0 - Unreleased

Cosy, in a world. Carpets you want to sit on, shaggy rugs, moss over stone, grass across a field. It grows real strands off the surface, so the pile has depth you can see from the side instead of being a picture of fur painted on a plane.

Added

  • Real Fur, for a room. Standing strands stacked in thin layers. Length and Density set the coat, Tufting clumps it into locks, and Droop and Wind Sway make it hang and move. Use the mask to grow it only where you need it. It builds real geometry, so keep Strands Per Face and Curve Segments as low as still looks good.
  • Surface Fur underneath it. A painted-on fibre highlight that costs nothing and fills in the backing between the strands, so the pile does not thin out into bare floor when you look straight down at it.
  • It keeps the cloth sheen that Fur Pro drops, so a rug can be velvet. Everything else the avatar shaders do is here too: the shading styles, matcaps, Car Paint, glitter, emissions, decals, outlines and the special effects.
  • LTCGI, Light Volumes, VRSL and AudioLink. All four, running together. LTCGI is the one that changes most in a world: your emissive surfaces become real area lights, which is what its author built it for.
  • Lighting Check. Under DEBUG TOOLS. Paints the surface with the light actually reaching it, so you can see which system your world is lighting it with. Set it back to Off before you upload.
  • Your baked lighting is read and used. Mark your geometry Contribute GI, press Generate Lighting, and the shader reads the result. Directional lightmaps mean your normal maps still catch the baked light instead of going flat, and shadowmask lets baked and real-time shadows sit together.   • Soft shadows and light cookies. The main directional light can cast soft shadows, point lights cast shadows now, and a light with a cookie projects it properly.
  • Smooth Filter, under Baked Lighting. Reads the baked lightmap with a smoother filter, so a low resolution bake stops looking blocky. On by default. It costs a few extra reads and no extra memory.

  • Reflection Clamp, under LIGHTING. Eases down a blown highlight coming off reflection probes, Light Volumes, LTCGI or stage lighting, with a slider each so you can leave one source out of it. Its neighbour, Specular Highlight Clamp, does the same for your scene lights. Both are off by default.

  • The first lock after a bake takes a while. Supporting lightmaps, shadowmask and soft shadows means more shader variants to build. It is working, not stuck, so let it finish.
  • No Wet or Goo here, on purpose. A carpet has no use for them and the textures are not free, so they are left out to keep the shader lighter. World Surface has both if you want a wet floor next to the rug.
VixForge / Light Baker 1.0.0 - Unreleased

A light baker for VRChat worlds that runs on your graphics card. Point it at a scene, press Bake, and it fills in Unity's own lightmaps, light probes and Light Volumes. First release as a product of its own.

Added

  • Bakes on your graphics card. A path tracer in a compute shader, with its own acceleration structure built over the scene, so a bake is a coffee rather than an evening.
  • It writes into Unity's own slots. The result goes where Unity keeps its lightmaps, so the world carries nothing extra and there is nothing for VRChat to strip on upload. A world lit with this uploads exactly like a world lit with Unity's lightmapper.
  • Directional lightmaps. The bake stores which way the light arrived, so normal maps still catch baked light instead of going flat. Turn it off for smaller files.
  • Light probes, for everything that moves through the scene, players included. Probe Samples sets their quality and Dilate Invalid Probes fills in the ones that landed inside geometry.
  • VRC Light Volumes are baked from the same trace, so your volumes agree with your lightmaps instead of being baked separately and drifting apart. Denoise Volumes cleans them, and Give Each Light Volume Its Own Name sorts out two volumes sharing a name and overwriting each other.
  • Terrain. A Terrain gets its own lightmap, casts shadows and bounces its colour onto everything around it. Its colour comes from the splat maps, so a sandy patch bounces sand and a grassy one bounces green. Terrain Detail sets how finely the bake reads the shape: raise it if terrain shadows look blocky, lower it if a big terrain makes the bake slow.
  • Emissive materials light the room. The baker aims rays straight at the glowing surfaces in your scene instead of waiting for a bounce to find one, so a neon sign or a screen actually lights what is near it rather than showing up as speckle. Set Global Illumination to Baked on the material for it to count, and Emitted Light beside it sets how bright it is in the bake.
  • Make Missing Lightmap UVs. Finds everything in the scene that can receive a lightmap but has no lightmap UVs, and gives it some. Models get Generate Lightmap UVs switched on and reimported. Anything with no import settings to hold that, like a mesh built in script, gets a copy with the UVs unwrapped, saved under Assets/VixenTools/BakedMeshes. It says what it is about to do and waits, because a reimport is not something Ctrl+Z puts back.
  • Ambient occlusion, with its own distance, for the creases and corners a bounce alone leaves flat.
  • Denoising, through OpenImageDenoise, Optix or Radeon. Pick one under Backend and it falls back to the others if that one is not available on your machine. Set it to None to see the raw trace.
  • Albedo Boost is read from your Lighting window, so how much colour bounces between surfaces matches what Unity's lightmapper would give you.
  • Sky Colour and Sky Intensity light the scene where nothing else does, and Indirect Intensity scales the bounce on its own without touching direct light.
  • Clear puts every renderer back to no lightmap, so a bake you do not like is one button to undo.
  • The bake report says what was skipped and why, which is the fastest way to find the one object that did not get lit.

Notes

  • An object set to receive light from Light Probes still casts shadows and still bounces its colour into the bake. So does one with its Lightmap Scale at 0. That is exactly how you tell Unity an object should light the room without taking lightmap space of its own, and the report does not call these left out.
  • Lightmap Scale on a renderer does what the field says, so raising it on one wall sharpens the shadows there without costing you resolution everywhere else.
  • Worlds only. It bakes a scene, so there is nothing for it to do on an avatar.
