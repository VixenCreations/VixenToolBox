VixForge / Light Baker 1.0.0 - Unreleased

A light baker for VRChat worlds that runs on your graphics card. Point it at a scene, press Bake, and it fills in Unity's own lightmaps, light probes and Light Volumes. First release as a product of its own.

Added

  • Bakes on your graphics card. A path tracer in a compute shader, with its own acceleration structure built over the scene, so a bake is a coffee rather than an evening.
  • It writes into Unity's own slots. The result goes where Unity keeps its lightmaps, so the world carries nothing extra and there is nothing for VRChat to strip on upload. A world lit with this uploads exactly like a world lit with Unity's lightmapper.
  • Directional lightmaps. The bake stores which way the light arrived, so normal maps still catch baked light instead of going flat. Turn it off for smaller files.
  • Light probes, for everything that moves through the scene, players included, in a scene that already holds Unity's probe data. Only Unity's lightmapper creates that data, so in a new scene Light Volumes light what moves. Probe Samples sets their quality and Dilate Invalid Probes fills in the ones that landed inside geometry.
  • VRC Light Volumes are baked from the same trace, so your volumes agree with your lightmaps instead of being baked separately and drifting apart. Denoise Volumes cleans them, and Give Each Light Volume Its Own Name sorts out two volumes sharing a name and overwriting each other.
  • Terrain. A Terrain gets its own lightmap, casts shadows and bounces its colour onto everything around it. Its colour comes from the splat maps, so a sandy patch bounces sand and a grassy one bounces green. Terrain Detail sets how finely the bake reads the shape: raise it if terrain shadows look blocky, lower it if a big terrain makes the bake slow.
  • Emissive materials light the room. The baker aims rays straight at the glowing surfaces in your scene instead of waiting for a bounce to find one, so a neon sign or a screen actually lights what is near it rather than showing up as speckle. Set Global Illumination to Baked on the material for it to count, and Emitted Light beside it sets how bright it is in the bake. The report names any material that glows but is not set to Baked.
  • Make Missing Lightmap UVs. Finds everything in the scene that can receive a lightmap but has no lightmap UVs, and gives it some. Models get Generate Lightmap UVs switched on and reimported. Anything with no import settings to hold that, like a mesh built in script, gets a copy with the UVs unwrapped, saved under Assets/VixenTools/BakedMeshes. It says what it is about to do and waits, because a reimport is not something Ctrl+Z puts back.
  • Ambient occlusion, with its own distance, for the creases and corners a bounce alone leaves flat.
  • Denoising, through OpenImageDenoise, Optix or Radeon. Pick one under Backend and it falls back to the others if that one is not available on your machine. Set it to None to see the raw trace.
  • Albedo Boost is read from your Lighting window, so how much colour bounces between surfaces matches what Unity's lightmapper would give you.
  • Shares its settings with your Lighting window. Texels Per Unit, Max Atlas Size, Padding, Samples, Bounces, Directional Mode, Ambient Occlusion, AO Distance, Indirect Intensity and the denoiser follow the scene's Lighting Settings: change one in either window and the other shows it. A scene with no Lighting Settings keeps the baker's own values.
  • Texels Per Unit goes up to 512, for close-up detail like a small test room.
  • Lights match Unity's lightmapper. Intensity, Range, the spot cone, Indirect Multiplier and soft shadows (only for lights set to Soft Shadows) bake the way Unity bakes them, so a scene lit for Unity's lightmapper looks the same here.
  • Area lights. Rectangle and Disc lights bake, with shadows as soft as the light is big, and they light your Light Volumes and light probes too.
  • Clean, smooth shadow edges. Every ray starts from a different spot across its texel, so shadow edges come out smooth instead of stepped, and the rays spread in an even pattern that clears up noise much faster than random ones.
  • Soft baked shadows from your lights. Baked Shadow Angle on a directional light and Baked Shadow Radius on a point or spot light soften the baked shadow, the same as in Unity's lightmapper.
  • Normal maps follow your baked lights. Directional lightmaps now include light straight from your lamps, not only the bounce, so bumps catch the light that actually hits them.
  • Uses your Lightmap Parameters. Pushoff, Backface Tolerance, Anti-aliasing Samples, Baked Tag and Limit Lightmap Count work the way they do with Unity's lightmapper, from the scene's Lighting Settings or a renderer's own.
  • Match To Bake for Post Processing. After each bake, a copy of each Post-process Volume's profile gets Auto Exposure and Bloom tuned to the new lighting. Your own profile is never changed.
  • Sky Colour and Sky Intensity light the scene where nothing else does, and Indirect Intensity scales the bounce on its own without touching direct light.
  • Clear puts every renderer back to no lightmap, so a bake you do not like is one button to undo. It leaves Unity's lighting data alone, so the scene keeps its light probes.
  • The bake report says what was skipped and why, which is the fastest way to find the one object that did not get lit.
  • Big scenes keep their detail. When a scene does not fit on one lightmap, the baker makes more lightmaps instead of shrinking everything, so every object bakes at the Texels Per Unit you set. The report says how many it made, and names any single object that is too big for one lightmap.
  • Long bakes run in short steps. The work goes to your graphics card in pieces sized from how fast the last ones ran, so a big scene or a high Samples count never holds the card long enough for Windows to reset the graphics driver. The progress bar has a Cancel button, and a cancelled bake leaves the scene as it was.
  • Knows how much graphics memory you have. When a lightmap at Max Atlas Size would not fit in your graphics card's memory while it bakes, the baker bakes smaller lightmaps at the same detail and tells you it did.

Notes

  • Use this baker or Unity's lightmapper on a project, never both. The two fight over the scene's global illumination data and the editor crashes. The window says so at the top, will not bake while Unity's lightmapper is baking or Auto Generate is on, and stops Unity's lightmapper if it starts on a scene baked here. Press Clear before you take a scene back to Unity's lightmapper.
  • An object set to receive light from Light Probes still casts shadows and still bounces its colour into the bake. So does one with its Lightmap Scale at 0. That is exactly how you tell Unity an object should light the room without taking lightmap space of its own, and the report does not call these left out.
  • Lightmap Scale on a renderer does what the field says, so raising it on one wall sharpens the shadows there without costing you resolution everywhere else.
  • It bakes scenes, not avatars, and it knows which project it is in. In a world project everything it bakes uploads with the world. In an avatar project it lights your test scene, so you can see your avatar under baked light, light probes, Light Volumes and reflections. Nothing from it uploads with an avatar there, the window says so, and anything on an avatar stays out of the bake and out of Make Missing Lightmap UVs.
