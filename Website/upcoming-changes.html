What is coming in the next release, written as it stands today. Nothing here has shipped yet, so any of it may still change before it does. Everything already released is on the Shader Changelogs page.

VixenWear / Latex Ultra 5.5.1 - Unreleased

A Cull setting that works, Color Adjust, Alpha Options and decal alpha and hue from Poiyomi Toon, Minimum Brightness in every world, and Volume and Peak Hold that follow the music.

Heads up

  • Cull works now. The main draw ignored Cull and always drew both sides of every face, so Back and Front did nothing. It now follows the setting, and Back is the default, so a single-sided piece such as a skirt, a cape or a hair card shows nothing from behind. Set Cull to Off on those materials for the old look. The Switchboard can set it on many materials at once.

Added

  • Color Adjust under BASE. Changes the colour of your Albedo before any lighting: Hue Shift, Saturation, Chromatize, Brightness, Gamma, a Tint and a Color Grading gradient map, with a Mask to limit each one. It works like Color Adjust in Poiyomi Toon and keeps the same property names, so a material switched over from Poiyomi keeps those settings and animations made for that one drive this one.
  • Alpha Options under BASE, for Cutout, Fade and Transparent. Alpha Mod adds to the opacity everywhere or takes it away, Beat Alpha lets the music move it, Dithering breaks see-through areas into a fine pattern, and Distance Alpha, Fresnel Alpha and Angular Alpha fade the material by how far away you are, toward its middle or edges, and by viewing angle. With Alpha To Coverage on in Cutout, Sharpened and Mip Level Alpha Scale tune the edge. Outlines fade with the body. It works like Alpha Options in Poiyomi Toon and keeps the same property names.
  • Global Mask in Alpha Options. One mask image, and Alpha Mod, Distance Alpha, Fresnel Alpha and Angular Alpha can each use one of its channels to apply only where it is light, so sheer fabric can fade while the lace on it stays solid.
  • Alpha, Alpha Blend Mode and Hue Shift on every decal. Alpha fades a decal in and out, which a menu toggle can drive. Alpha Blend Mode lets a decal set how see-through the material is, everywhere or only inside the decal. Hue Shift turns the decal's colours round the colour wheel. They work like the same decal settings in Poiyomi Toon and keep the same property names.
  • Tiling Moves Albedo Only, under the Albedo. Turn it on and Tiling and Offset move only the Albedo while every other map stays where it is, the way Poiyomi and lilToon work. Off keeps things as they were.
  • More ways to dissolve. Cymatics is a new Shape that follows a vibrating plate figure the music can bend. Portal turns the dissolved part into a window onto Hyperspace, Inner Cosmos or Cymatics instead of a hole. Beat Travel runs each beat along the shape as a wave, with a Drop Burst on big peaks. Viewer sets a different Amount in mirrors and in the VRChat camera, for a body only a mirror shows. Proximity opens a window around whoever comes close, and Interior fills the cut with a solid colour so it reads as a solid cross section.
  • Okhsv in Hue Shift, in Color Adjust and on every decal. Every colour keeps its strength as it turns, so a strong red becomes an equally strong blue. With it, Colorize gives greys, whites and pale colours some of the hue, so the shift shows on them too. Black always stays black.
  • Shatter and Glitter Sparks in Dissolve. Shatter breaks the edge into shards that fly off, spin and fall as the material dissolves, with a glow and trails if you want them. Glitter Sparks lights your glitter up along the front, so each flake, bat or skull burns off as a spark when the edge passes.
  • Hologram under SPECIAL EFFECTS. Turns the material into a see-through projection that glows at the edges and pulses with the beat, coloured by a Palette that can run up the body or in from the edges, with rolling scanlines, static, a flicker and glitches that tear the body sideways. Five of the palettes are for Halloween. Set Rendering Mode to Transparent for the brightest glow.
  • Pearl under SPECIAL EFFECTS, made for hair. An aurora of colours that shifts as you look, a rainbow shimmer toward the edges, a shine that sits high on the hair, an edge colour, a tint and opacity for the backs of hair cards, and a Root To Tip gradient that tints from the roots to the tips while keeping the brightness of your texture.
  • Haunting in Alpha Options, for Halloween. Flicker wavers like a restless spirit and cuts out now and then, Heartbeat fades you in on each beat of a slow heart, Look Away makes you vanish wherever someone looks straight at you, Shy Of Light shows you only in the dark, and Mirrors hides you from mirrors like a vampire or shows you only there. Your shadow stays where you are.
  • Goo Melt in Dissolve. The material turns to goo just ahead of the front and runs off as it dissolves, with the sag, slime and drips you set under Goo Effects.

Fixed

  • Minimum Brightness works in every world. A world whose Light Volume Manager has Force Scene Lighting ticked switched it off, and after you pressed Play in a scene like that it stayed off in the editor until Unity restarted. It now always applies.
  • Volume and Peak Hold move with the music. With the AudioLink Mode on Classic, both sat near full for as long as music played, so Cymatics, Inner Cosmos and anything else set to them barely reacted. They now rise and fall with the track, and an effect's beat timing on either one follows the whole mix instead of Bass or Treble.
  • Point and spot lights on Fade materials. In Fade, each point and spot light lit the material at full strength however see-through it was, so a faint material glowed like a solid one under a lamp. Those lights now fade with it.
  • Hue Shift in OKLab keeps the hue you set. Turning a strong colour could push it past what a screen can show, and it then came out a noticeably different hue. It now lands on the hue you asked for and gives up only a little vividness where it must. Colours that already fit come out exactly as before. This covers Color Adjust and every decal's Hue Shift.

With Alpha To Coverage on in Cutout, Sharpened and Mip Level Alpha Scale tune the edge. Outlines fade with the body. It works like Alpha Options in Poiyomi Toon and keeps the same property names.
  • Global Mask in Alpha Options. One mask image, and Alpha Mod, Distance Alpha, Fresnel Alpha and Angular Alpha can each use one of its channels to apply only where it is light, so sheer fabric can fade while the lace on it stays solid.
  • Alpha, Alpha Blend Mode and Hue Shift on every decal. Alpha fades a decal in and out, which a menu toggle can drive. Alpha Blend Mode lets a decal set how see-through the material is, everywhere or only inside the decal. Hue Shift turns the decal's colours round the colour wheel. They work like the same decal settings in Poiyomi Toon and keep the same property names.
  • Tiling Moves Albedo Only, under the Albedo. Turn it on and Tiling and Offset move only the Albedo while every other map stays where it is, the way Poiyomi and lilToon work. Off keeps things as they were.
  • More ways to dissolve. Cymatics is a new Shape that follows a vibrating plate figure the music can bend. Portal turns the dissolved part into a window onto Hyperspace, Inner Cosmos or Cymatics instead of a hole. Beat Travel runs each beat along the shape as a wave, with a Drop Burst on big peaks. Viewer sets a different Amount in mirrors and in the VRChat camera, for a body only a mirror shows. Proximity opens a window around whoever comes close, and Interior fills the cut with a solid colour so it reads as a solid cross section.
  • Okhsv in Hue Shift, in Color Adjust and on every decal. Every colour keeps its strength as it turns, so a strong red becomes an equally strong blue. With it, Colorize gives greys, whites and pale colours some of the hue, so the shift shows on them too. Black always stays black.
  • Shatter and Glitter Sparks in Dissolve. Shatter breaks the edge into shards that fly off, spin and fall as the material dissolves, with a glow and trails if you want them. Glitter Sparks lights your glitter up along the front, so each flake, bat or skull burns off as a spark when the edge passes.
  • Hologram under SPECIAL EFFECTS. Turns the material into a see-through projection that glows at the edges and pulses with the beat, coloured by a Palette that can run up the body or in from the edges, with rolling scanlines, static, a flicker and glitches that tear the body sideways. Five of the palettes are for Halloween. Set Rendering Mode to Transparent for the brightest glow.
  • Pearl under SPECIAL EFFECTS, made for hair. An aurora of colours that shifts as you look, a rainbow shimmer toward the edges, a shine that sits high on the hair, an edge colour, a tint and opacity for the backs of hair cards, and a Root To Tip gradient that tints from the roots to the tips while keeping the brightness of your texture.
  • Haunting in Alpha Options, for Halloween. Flicker wavers like a restless spirit and cuts out now and then, Heartbeat fades you in on each beat of a slow heart, Look Away makes you vanish wherever someone looks straight at you, Shy Of Light shows you only in the dark, and Mirrors hides you from mirrors like a vampire or shows you only there. Your shadow stays where you are.

Fixed

  • Minimum Brightness works in every world. A world whose Light Volume Manager has Force Scene Lighting ticked switched it off, and after you pressed Play in a scene like that it stayed off in the editor until Unity restarted. It now always applies.
  • Volume and Peak Hold move with the music. With the AudioLink Mode on Classic, both sat near full for as long as music played, so Cymatics, Inner Cosmos and anything else set to them barely reacted. They now rise and fall with the track, and an effect's beat timing on either one follows the whole mix instead of Bass or Treble.
  • Point and spot lights on Fade materials. In Fade, each point and spot light lit the material at full strength however see-through it was, so a faint material glowed like a solid one under a lamp. Those lights now fade with it.
  • Hue Shift in OKLab keeps the hue you set. Turning a strong colour could push it past what a screen can show, and it then came out a noticeably different hue. It now lands on the hue you asked for and gives up only a little vividness where it must. Colours that already fit come out exactly as before. This covers Color Adjust and every decal's Hue Shift.
VixForge / Light Baker 1.0.0 - Unreleased

A light baker for VRChat worlds that runs on your graphics card. Point it at a scene, press Bake, and it fills in Unity's own lightmaps, light probes and Light Volumes. First release as a product of its own.

Added

  • Bakes on your graphics card. A path tracer in a compute shader, with its own acceleration structure built over the scene, so a bake is a coffee rather than an evening.
  • It writes into Unity's own slots. The result goes where Unity keeps its lightmaps, so the world carries nothing extra and there is nothing for VRChat to strip on upload. A world lit with this uploads exactly like a world lit with Unity's lightmapper.
  • Directional lightmaps. The bake stores which way the light arrived, so normal maps still catch baked light instead of going flat. Turn it off for smaller files.
  • Unified Light lightmaps, a Directional Mode for scenes lit with VixForge world materials. The direction is stored in VixForge's own form, which those materials read more accurately, so bumps follow your baked lamps more like real-time light. The baker turns on Unified Light Lightmaps on each VixForge material it bakes, and says which locked materials need locking again. Other shaders read it as an ordinary directional lightmap, a little flatter.
  • Environment Samples aim rays straight at the sky, so open ground outdoors comes out smooth without raising every other sample count.
  • Direct Filter and Indirect Filter, Gaussian or A-Trous, smooth the light after the bake the way Unity's Advanced filtering does, without reaching across to an object somewhere else in the scene.
  • Lightmap Compression sets how much the colour lightmaps are compressed. The direction lightmaps are never compressed.
  • Light probes of its own, for everything that moves through the scene, players included, on any shader. The baker builds Unity's light probe data itself, so a scene needs nothing from Unity's lightmapper, and Mixed lights shadow moving objects through the probes too. They carry all nine lighting terms Unity stores, the finer shading that VixForge's Unified Light reads. Place Light Probes lays them out for you on a grid, leaving out any inside walls and floors, and Probe Samples sets their quality.
  • VRC Light Volumes are baked from the same trace, so your volumes agree with your lightmaps instead of being baked separately and drifting apart. Denoise Volumes cleans them, Dilate Invalid Probes fills in the volume probes that landed inside geometry, and Give Each Light Volume Its Own Name sorts out two volumes sharing a name and overwriting each other. It bakes the volumes in the Light Volume Manager's list, and the report says when an Additive volume got every light in the scene, or when a volume with no baked lighting stops Light Volumes packing its atlas.
  • Terrain. A Terrain gets its own lightmap, casts shadows and bounces its colour onto everything around it. Its colour comes from the splat maps, so a sandy patch bounces sand and a grassy one bounces green. Terrain Detail sets how finely the bake reads the shape: raise it if terrain shadows look blocky, lower it if a big terrain makes the bake slow.
  • Emissive materials light the room. The baker aims rays straight at the glowing surfaces in your scene instead of waiting for a bounce to find one, so a neon sign or a screen actually lights what is near it rather than showing up as speckle. Set Global Illumination to Baked on the material for it to count, and Emitted Light beside it sets how bright it is in the bake. The report names any material that glows but is not set to Baked.
  • Make Missing Lightmap UVs. Finds everything in the scene that can receive a lightmap but has no lightmap UVs, and gives it some. Models get Generate Lightmap UVs switched on and reimported. Anything with no import settings to hold that, like a mesh built in script, gets a copy with the UVs unwrapped, saved under Assets/VixenTools/BakedMeshes. It says what it is about to do and waits, because a reimport is not something Ctrl+Z puts back.
  • Ambient occlusion, with its own distance, for the creases and corners a bounce alone leaves flat. AO On Direct Light and AO On Bounced Light set how much it darkens each, and start where Unity's do: bounced light only.
  • Denoising, through OpenImageDenoise, Optix or Radeon. It cleans the bounced light and leaves the light straight from your lights alone, so shadow edges stay sharp. Big lightmaps go through in tiles, so the denoiser fits in your graphics card's memory. Pick one under Backend and it falls back to the others if that one is not available on your machine. Set it to None to see the raw trace.
  • Albedo Boost is read from your Lighting window, so how much colour bounces between surfaces matches what Unity's lightmapper would give you.
  • Shares its settings with your Lighting window. Texels Per Unit, Max Atlas Size, Padding, Lightmap Compression, Samples, Direct Samples, Environment Samples, Probe Samples (as the Light Probe Sample Multiplier), Bounces, Min Bounces, Directional Mode, Ambient Occlusion, AO Distance, AO On Direct Light, AO On Bounced Light, Indirect Intensity, the denoiser and the direct and indirect filters follow the scene's Lighting Settings: change one in either window and the other shows it. A scene with no Lighting Settings keeps the baker's own values.
  • Texels Per Unit goes up to 512, for close-up detail like a small test room.
  • Lights match Unity's lightmapper. Intensity, Range, the spot cone, Indirect Multiplier and soft shadows (only for lights set to Soft Shadows) bake the way Unity bakes them, so a scene lit for Unity's lightmapper looks the same here.
  • Area lights. Rectangle and Disc lights bake, with shadows as soft as the light is big, and they light your Light Volumes and light probes too.
  • Clean, smooth shadow edges. Every ray starts from a different spot across its texel, so shadow edges come out smooth instead of stepped, and the rays spread in an even pattern that clears up noise much faster than random ones.
  • Soft baked shadows from your lights. Baked Shadow Angle on a directional light and Baked Shadow Radius on a point or spot light soften the baked shadow, the same as in Unity's lightmapper.
  • Normal maps follow your baked lights. Directional lightmaps now include light straight from your lamps, not only the bounce, so bumps catch the light that actually hits them.
  • Uses your Lightmap Parameters. Pushoff, Backface Tolerance, Anti-aliasing Samples, Baked Tag and Limit Lightmap Count work the way they do with Unity's lightmapper, from the scene's Lighting Settings or a renderer's own.
  • Match To Bake for Post Processing. After each bake, a copy of each Post-process Volume's profile gets Auto Exposure and Bloom tuned to the new lighting. Your own profile is never changed.
  • Sky Colour and Sky Intensity light the scene where nothing else does, and Indirect Intensity scales the bounce on its own without touching direct light.
  • Clear puts every renderer back to no lightmap, so a bake you do not like is one button to undo. It leaves Unity's lighting data alone, so the scene keeps its light probes. The bake's files go to the Recycle Bin.
  • Bakes tidy up after themselves. Files in the scene's bake folder that a new bake no longer uses, such as a lightmap from a bigger earlier bake, go to the Recycle Bin.
  • The bake report says what was skipped and why, which is the fastest way to find the one object that did not get lit.
  • Big scenes keep their detail. When a scene does not fit on one lightmap, the baker makes more lightmaps instead of shrinking everything, so every object bakes at the Texels Per Unit you set. The report says how many it made, and names any single object that is too big for one lightmap.
  • Long bakes run in short steps. The work goes to your graphics card in pieces sized from how fast the last ones ran, so a big scene or a high Samples count never holds the card long enough for Windows to reset the graphics driver. GPU Use sets how much of the card a bake takes: Balanced keeps your desktop smooth, Full is fastest and Light leaves the computer free for other work. The progress bar has a Cancel button all the way through. Cancelled before the lightmaps are applied, the scene stays as it was; cancelled during the probes or volumes, the lightmaps are in and the rest keep their old lighting.
  • Busy scenes bake quickly. Every probe and Light Volume voxel shares its rays across the whole graphics card, each bounce checks one light picked by how much it would add instead of testing every light, and the scene is sorted so each ray looks at far less of it. Place Light Probes only checks whether each spot is inside something, so it no longer bakes the lighting of spots it throws away.
  • Direct Samples sets the rays aimed at your lights apart from Samples, because light straight from a lamp needs fewer rays than the bounce. Min Bounces traces every bounce up to it and lets dim paths past it stop early, so deep bounces cost less and the light comes out the same.
  • Knows how much graphics memory you have. When a lightmap at Max Atlas Size would not fit in your graphics card's memory while it bakes, the baker bakes smaller lightmaps at the same detail and tells you it did.
  • Bake Regions and Bake Changes. Bake Regions rebakes the lightmaps, Light Probes, Light Volume voxels and reflection probes inside the boxes you tick, and leaves the rest of the scene as it is. Bake Changes rebakes only what changed since the last bake: a moved light is traced again over its reach, and a moved object over the space around it. An adjustable light that only changed colour, Intensity or Indirect Multiplier, or was switched on or off, needs no tracing at all. Edge Blend fades the new light into the old at the edges.

Notes

  • A scene is lit by this baker or by Unity's lightmapper, never both at once. The two fight over the scene's global illumination data and the editor crashes. The window says so at the top and will not bake while Unity's lightmapper is baking or Auto Generate is on. Press Generate Lighting on a scene baked here and the baker hands it over: it clears its own bake, closes every other open scene, and starts Unity's bake again.
  • An object set to receive light from Light Probes still casts shadows and still bounces its colour into the bake. So does one with its Lightmap Scale at 0. That is exactly how you tell Unity an object should light the room without taking lightmap space of its own, and the report does not call these left out.
  • Lightmap Scale on a renderer does what the field says, so raising it on one wall sharpens the shadows there without costing you resolution everywhere else.
  • It bakes scenes, not avatars, and it knows which project it is in. In a world project everything it bakes uploads with the world. In an avatar project it lights your test scene, so you can see your avatar under baked light, light probes, Light Volumes and reflections. Nothing from it uploads with an avatar there, the window says so, and anything on an avatar stays out of the bake and out of Make Missing Lightmap UVs.
VixForge Editor 2.6.1 - Unreleased

Shader Hub tools close when you switch away, so they stop using memory, and materials from Poiyomi, lilToon and VRChat's Toon Standard come across with their metallic maps, decals and much more.

Heads up

  • Shader Hub tools close when you switch away. Switching to another tool, or to the documents, now closes the one you were in, and anything you had not applied there is lost. Apply your changes before you switch, or turn off Close Hub Tools When You Switch in the VixForge Editor settings to keep every tool as you left it.

Added

  • Close Hub Tools When You Switch, in the VixForge Editor settings under Technical. On by default. A tool you leave in the Shader Hub closes and frees its memory, where before every tool you opened stayed in memory until you closed the Hub.

Changed

  • Translating from Poiyomi, lilToon and VRChat's Toon Standard is rebuilt. Translate Materials and the Shader Translation button at the top of the inspector now bring across decals (lilToon's second and third main colours too), every emission and matcap layer, occlusion, detail normals, parallax, alpha maps, outlines, the rendering mode, Color Adjust, Alpha Options with its Global Mask, Dissolve, UV Tile Discard and each decal's alpha and hue shift, onto any VixForge shader. Materials Poiyomi has locked translate too. Menu toggles and animations that drive these settings keep working, because the names match. Anything with no match is listed in the Console for each material.

Fixed

  • Metallic maps come across from Poiyomi. The translation read Poiyomi's Stylized Specular settings instead of Reflections & Specular, so the metallic map was lost and every material came out at full smoothness. The packed map now lands in the Packed PBR Mask with its channels, inverts and reflection and specular masks. A material with Reflections & Specular off starts at Smoothness 0, so it stays matte the way it was.
  • Metallic and gloss maps from lilToon and VRChat's Toon Standard go to Separate Maps. They were put in the Packed PBR Mask, which read the wrong channels from them.
  • Poiyomi's rendering preset comes across. TransClipping, Additive and Multiplicative landed on a rendering mode that does not exist here.
  • Translated materials show everything they were given. After Translate Materials, occlusion, metallic and smoothness maps, masks and glow could stay switched off inside the shader until you opened each material in the inspector. This also hit materials from Unity Standard and REDSIM. They now work straight away, and so does the Set Up for Poiyomi / Mochie Metallic Map button.
  • A translated material keeps its maps lined up. Poiyomi and lilToon move only the main texture with its Tiling, and here every map followed it, so a normal map or mask could shrink to a speck. Translations now turn on Tiling Moves Albedo Only when the material needs it, and decals stay where they were placed.
  • Features stay switched on where you set them. A feature turned on by a preset, a tool, a translation or a script could stay switched off inside the shader until you opened the material, and locking could keep it that way. Every VixForge shader now updates on its own.
