Upcoming Changes

What is coming in the next release, written as it stands today. Nothing here has shipped yet, so any of it may still change before it does. Everything already released is on the Shader Changelogs page.

VixenWear / Latex Ultra 5.6.1 - Unreleased

Hypno that takes over your own view, eyes that hypnotise, follow and glint, Sheer & Lace with new fabrics for procedural latex, matcaps you build yourself, Halloween presets, outlines that move with your body, with four new ways to draw them, latex that reacts as it stretches, and a rebuilt Viewer Context with Hidden Ink.

Heads up

  • Viewer Context has its own section now, VIEWER CONTEXT, just above AUDIOLINK. Your settings move over by themselves. If an animation drives an old Viewer Context setting, point it at the matching setting in the new section.

Added

  • Silhouette Ripples under OUTLINES. Rings peel off your outline and travel outward, fading as they go, like sound waves or heat coming off you. Distance, Rings, Ring Width, Softness, Fade and Speed set the look. With AudioLink, Beat Launch sends every hit in the music out as its own ring, and Beat Speed pushes them faster on the beat.
  • Ink Weight under OUTLINES. Your outline gets heavier where your body turns away from the light and lighter where the light hits it, the way an inker draws a line. It follows the room's main light and light probes, so the weight shifts as you walk past a lamp.
  • Line Boil under OUTLINES. The hand-drawn wobble of classic cartoons: your outline redraws itself a few times a second, a little different each time. Amount, Scale and Frame Rate set how much it moves, how close the wobbles sit and how often it redraws.
  • Velocity Smear under OUTLINES. When you move, your outline streaks out behind you like the speed lines in an anime, and snaps back when you stop. Streaks breaks the trail into separate lines. It uses the Velocity you set up in Movement under SPECIAL EFFECTS.
  • Hypno under SPECIAL EFFECTS. A spiral that takes over your own view: the world twists toward the middle and the spiral swims in over it. Hypno Setup in the Shader Hub attaches it to your avatar with a menu toggle, and only you see it, unless you let others join and someone leans inside Radius. Pick Spiral, Endless Spiral, Rings or Pinwheel, add Rainbow and Pulse, and let AudioLink spin it, throb it and colour it. A ready-made Hypno material comes in VixForge/Materials as a guide to start from.
  • Eyes under SPECIAL EFFECTS, for a material on an eye. Line Iris Centres and Iris Size up with the irises in your texture, then turn on any of four effects. Hypno Iris turns a spiral or rings inside the iris and can cycle the rainbow; give it Hypno's Speed and they turn together. Pupil Shapes draws a heart, star, cat slit, cross, diamond or spiral pupil that opens wider in the dark and pulses with the beat. Watcher Eyes turns the iris to look at whoever is looking at you, mirrors included. Deep Iris sets the iris deep under a glassy cornea, with a dark rim and a glint that can follow the light.
  • Sheer & Lace under SPECIAL EFFECTS, brought over from Clothing Pro so you can make procedural latex: see-through fabric that thickens at the edges, nets from fishnet to tulle, lace, and every new fabric with it: knits, weaves, sports mesh, chainmail, perforated, prints, quilting and distress.
  • Procedural matcaps you build yourself, on any matcap layer set to Procedural. Twelve new styles under Style: Chladni Plate, Drum Head, Ripple Tank, Hyperspace Tunnel, Hyperspace Weave, Grid, Rings, Starfield, Spectrum, Waveform, Julia Fractal and Nebula. Space folds any style into a kaleidoscope, a tunnel, a spiral or a mirror, with Space Shape setting segments, twist, flow and spin. Colour turns the pattern into two or three colours, a rainbow, a palette or the colour of the music, and Colour Shape adds bands, invert, glow and cycling. With AudioLink, Pattern Beat and Pattern Beat 2 let the music grow, warp, push, twist, shake and reshape the pattern, send each hit out as a ring, or lay the spectrum across it.
  • Packed Atlas reaches all 16 cells of a 4 by 4 sheet, where it stopped at 8, with Sections up to 16 and eight more section looks in Atlas Sections.
  • Halloween presets. Eleven looks under Halloween in Presets, each adding one effect on top of what you already have. Bat Swarm, Pumpkin Patch, Skull Scatter and Haunted Night scatter glitter figures over you, and Jack-o'-Lantern makes Emission Layer 1 flicker like a candle. Ghost, Witching Hour Hologram, Restless Spirit and Vampire make you see-through, a hologram, a flickering spirit or missing from mirrors. They switch Rendering Mode to Fade or Transparent, and Vampire to Cutout. Dissolve Embers and Dissolve Melt dress up a dissolve you have already set up.
  • Stretch Response under SURFACE. Latex reacts to how your mesh stretches as you move: it goes paler and glossier where it pulls tight, your normal map detail flattens there and sharpens where it bunches, and creases fold in where it squashes. Put your avatar in its rest pose, run Stretch Bake in the Shader Hub under Tools > Mesh Fixes, then turn it on. Show Stretch colours the material so you can check the bake. It follows avatar scaling in game.
  • Hidden Ink in VIEWER CONTEXT. A design that only the right light reveals, like UV-reactive ink: pick its Light Colour and it glows wherever that colour of light lands on it. In Glow or Paint mode it shows by any rule you like instead, such as only in mirrors or only up close.
  • Light Data, a new group at the top of SHADING, picks which lights reach you. Turn off Pixel Lights or Vertex Lights to ignore the world's extra lights, and a locked material leaves them out, so it locks faster. Point Light Shadows, Soft Shadows and Light Cookies let point lights shadow you, give spot and point light shadows soft edges, and project a point light's or sun's cookie onto you. They start off, since each one adds lock time.
  • Enable on Rim Light, Subsurface, Transmission, Anisotropy, Micro Lighting, Cavity Lighting and Micro Shading. Untick one you don't use and a locked material leaves it out, so locking is faster.
  • TOUCH, a new section just above AUDIOLINK, lets real hands, fingers and heads touch you through VRChat's Material Contacts, so a touch lands exactly where it happens, in mirrors too. Turn on Material Contacts, pick which kinds of touch count, and run Touch Setup in the Shader Hub to add the contact receiver. It needs a VRChat SDK with Write to Shaders on contact receivers, and Touch Setup tells you if yours is too old. Touch Glow lights you up where you are touched: a soft Glow, rings that Ripple out, or a Ring round each touch, with Colour Per Person and Who Glows.
  • Contacts in Touch under SPECIAL EFFECTS. The goo dents, bulges, squeezes out tendrils and goes wet under real hands from TOUCH, with no zones to paint and no animator, and Touch Wipe clears the water there too.

Changed

  • Light Volumes is up to date with 3.0.1, its first full 3.0 release.
  • Faster shader compiles. Each part of the shader no longer builds its own copy for settings it never uses, so there is less to compile after an update and when you change a material. Outlines, and the Vertex Fracture shards build fewer copies of themselves, and a motion pass VRChat never used is gone.
  • Best Soft, Wet Look and Soft Silicone presets re-tuned. The rim on Best Soft and Wet Look is a thin edge again, where it had spread across almost half of each limb. Soft Silicone sets Highlight Clamp to 0.25, so its shine stays soft now that highlights from lights are as bright as in Unity's own shaders.
  • Viewer Context rebuilt. Every part of the material has its own rules now, each in its own spot under VIEWER CONTEXT: Base, Normal Maps, Surface, Shading, Decals, Polish, Special Effects, Outlines, Emissions, Matcaps and AudioLink. As well as who is looking, where and how close, a rule can follow the colour of the light on you, how dark it is, the beat, or a Gaze Torch that shows a part only where someone looks.

Fixed

  • Outlines move with your body. When Writhe, the goo sag, Oobleck Dance, Slosh, Splash, Touch, Displacement Strength, the AudioLink Kinetic Modules, VRSL movement or a Hologram glitch moved your body, the outline stayed where you had been, so your body poked through the line or left it behind. It now moves with all of them.
  • The face mirror counts as a mirror. Viewer Context, Mirrors in Haunting and the mirror Amount in Dissolve's Viewer now read VRChat's face mirror flag as well, so your face mirror treats you the way other mirrors do.
  • Colour By Time Of Day works in worlds without AudioLink, taking the time from VRChat itself, and the colour now drifts smoothly through the day instead of stepping once an hour.
  • Sonar and Infinity Mirror line up in both eyes in VR. Sonar from Whoever Is Looking and the Infinity Mirror's Opens Up Close were measured from each eye separately, so each eye saw slightly different rings and depth.
  • Native Touch skips the face mirror, as it already skipped other mirrors.
  • Shadows follow the Hologram glitch. When the Hologram glitch moved your body, its shadow stayed put. The shadow now moves with it.
  • UV Tile Discard turns off when an animation turns it off. With Enable marked Animated, the tiles stayed hidden after the animation switched it off.
  • Point light shadows start at the object. The shadow this material casts from a point light used the setup meant for spot and sun shadows, which pushed it away from the object a second time. It now uses Unity's own point light shadow setup.

VixForge / Light Baker 1.0.0 - Unreleased

A light baker for VRChat worlds that runs on your graphics card. Point it at a scene, press Bake, and it fills in Unity's own lightmaps, light probes and Light Volumes. First release as a product of its own.

Added

  • Bakes on your graphics card. A path tracer in a compute shader, with its own acceleration structure built over the scene, so a bake is a coffee rather than an evening.
  • It writes into Unity's own slots. The result goes where Unity keeps its lightmaps, so the world carries nothing extra and there is nothing for VRChat to strip on upload. A world lit with this uploads exactly like a world lit with Unity's lightmapper.
  • Directional lightmaps. The bake stores which way the light arrived, so normal maps still catch baked light instead of going flat. It picks the direction that best matches how the light really changes across a bumpy surface, so bumps follow your baked lamps far more like real-time light, on any shader. Turn it off for smaller files.
  • Unified Light lightmaps, a Directional Mode for scenes lit with VixForge world materials. The direction is stored in VixForge's own form, which those materials read more accurately, so bumps follow your baked lamps more like real-time light. The baker turns on Unified Light Lightmaps on each VixForge material it bakes, and says which locked materials need locking again. Other shaders read it as an ordinary directional lightmap, a little flatter.
  • Environment Samples aim rays straight at the sky, so open ground outdoors comes out smooth without raising every other sample count.
  • Direct Filter and Indirect Filter, Gaussian or A-Trous, smooth the light after the bake the way Unity's Advanced filtering does, without reaching across to an object somewhere else in the scene.
  • Lightmap Compression sets how much the colour lightmaps are compressed. The direction lightmaps are never compressed.
  • Light probes of its own, for everything that moves through the scene, players included, on any shader. The baker builds Unity's light probe data itself, so a scene needs nothing from Unity's lightmapper, and Mixed lights shadow moving objects through the probes too. They carry all nine lighting terms Unity stores, the finer shading that VixForge's Unified Light reads. Place Light Probes lays them out for you on a grid, leaving out any inside walls and floors, and Probe Samples sets their quality.
  • VRC Light Volumes are baked from the same trace, so your volumes agree with your lightmaps instead of being baked separately and drifting apart. Denoise Volumes cleans them, Dilate Invalid Probes fills in the volume probes that landed inside geometry, and Give Each Light Volume Its Own Name sorts out two volumes sharing a name and overwriting each other. It bakes the volumes in the Light Volume Manager's list, and the report says when an Additive volume got every light in the scene, or when a volume with no baked lighting stops Light Volumes packing its atlas.
  • Terrain. A Terrain gets its own lightmap, casts shadows and bounces its colour onto everything around it. Its colour comes from the splat maps, so a sandy patch bounces sand and a grassy one bounces green. Terrain Detail sets how finely the bake reads the shape: raise it if terrain shadows look blocky, lower it if a big terrain makes the bake slow.
  • Emissive materials light the room. The baker aims rays straight at the glowing surfaces in your scene instead of waiting for a bounce to find one, so a neon sign or a screen actually lights what is near it rather than showing up as speckle. A big glowing panel right beside a wall comes out clean too, because the rays that find it on their own count alongside the aimed ones. A glowing surface lights only what is in front of it, unless its material has Double Sided Global Illumination on. Set Global Illumination to Baked on the material for it to count, and Emitted Light beside it sets how bright it is in the bake. The report names any material that glows but is not set to Baked.
  • Make Missing Lightmap UVs. Finds everything in the scene that can receive a lightmap but has no lightmap UVs, and gives it some. Models get Generate Lightmap UVs switched on and reimported. Anything with no import settings to hold that, like a mesh built in script, gets a copy with the UVs unwrapped, saved under Assets/VixenTools/BakedMeshes. It says what it is about to do and waits, because a reimport is not something Ctrl+Z puts back.
  • Ambient occlusion, with its own distance, for the creases and corners a bounce alone leaves flat. AO On Direct Light and AO On Bounced Light set how much it darkens each, and start where Unity's do: bounced light only.
  • Denoising, through OpenImageDenoise, Optix or Radeon. It cleans the bounced light and leaves the light straight from your lights alone, so shadow edges stay sharp. Big lightmaps go through in tiles, so the denoiser fits in your graphics card's memory. Pick one under Backend and it falls back to the others if that one is not available on your machine. Set it to None to see the raw trace.
  • Albedo Boost is read from your Lighting window, so how much colour bounces between surfaces matches what Unity's lightmapper would give you.
  • Shares its settings with your Lighting window. Texels Per Unit, Max Atlas Size, Padding, Lightmap Compression, Samples, Direct Samples, Environment Samples, Probe Samples (as the Light Probe Sample Multiplier), Bounces, Min Bounces, Directional Mode, Ambient Occlusion, AO Distance, AO On Direct Light, AO On Bounced Light, Indirect Intensity, the denoiser and the direct and indirect filters follow the scene's Lighting Settings: change one in either window and the other shows it. A scene with no Lighting Settings keeps the baker's own values.
  • Texels Per Unit goes up to 512, for close-up detail like a small test room.
  • Lights match Unity's lightmapper. Intensity, Range, the spot cone, Indirect Multiplier and soft shadows (only for lights set to Soft Shadows) bake the way Unity bakes them, so a scene lit for Unity's lightmapper looks the same here.
  • Area lights. Rectangle and Disc lights bake, with shadows as soft as the light is big, and they light your Light Volumes and light probes too. The light they bounce stays right even on a surface right next to the panel.
  • Clean, smooth shadow edges. Every ray starts from a different spot across its texel, so shadow edges come out smooth instead of stepped, and the rays spread in an even pattern that clears up noise much faster than random ones.
  • Seams stitched. Where a model's lightmap UVs are cut but the surface itself is smooth, the light on both sides of the cut is matched, so the cut does not show as a line. It follows Stitch Seams on each renderer, like Unity's lightmapper, and hard edges keep their own light.
  • No light leaking under walls. A lightmap pixel that sits partly under a wall is moved out to the open side, so a floor does not pick up light or shadow from the far side of the wall it touches.
  • Soft baked shadows from your lights. Baked Shadow Angle on a directional light and Baked Shadow Radius on a point or spot light soften the baked shadow, the same as in Unity's lightmapper.
  • Normal maps follow your baked lights. Directional lightmaps now include light straight from your lamps, not only the bounce, so bumps catch the light that actually hits them.
  • Uses your Lightmap Parameters. Pushoff, Backface Tolerance, Anti-aliasing Samples, Baked Tag and Limit Lightmap Count work the way they do with Unity's lightmapper, from the scene's Lighting Settings or a renderer's own.
  • Match To Bake for Post Processing. After each bake, a copy of each Post-process Volume's profile gets Auto Exposure and Bloom tuned to the new lighting. Your own profile is never changed.
  • The sky comes from your scene. The baker lights with the Environment Lighting in your Lighting window: your skybox, gradient or colour, and its Intensity Multiplier, the same sky Unity's lightmapper uses. The report says which one it used. Indirect Intensity scales the bounce on its own without touching direct light.
  • Clear puts every renderer back to no lightmap, so a bake you do not like is one button to undo. It leaves Unity's lighting data alone, so the scene keeps its light probes. The bake's files go to the Recycle Bin.
  • Bakes tidy up after themselves. Files in the scene's bake folder that a new bake no longer uses, such as a lightmap from a bigger earlier bake, go to the Recycle Bin.
  • The bake report says what was skipped and why, which is the fastest way to find the one object that did not get lit.
  • Big scenes keep their detail. When a scene does not fit on one lightmap, the baker makes more lightmaps instead of shrinking everything, so every object bakes at the Texels Per Unit you set. The report says how many it made, and names any single object that is too big for one lightmap.
  • Long bakes run in short steps. The work goes to your graphics card in pieces sized from how fast the last ones ran, so a big scene or a high Samples count never holds the card long enough for Windows to reset the graphics driver. GPU Use sets how much of the card a bake takes: Balanced keeps your desktop smooth, Full is fastest and Light leaves the computer free for other work. The progress bar has a Cancel button all the way through. Cancelled before the lightmaps are applied, the scene stays as it was; cancelled during the probes or volumes, the lightmaps are in and the rest keep their old lighting.
  • Busy scenes bake quickly. Every probe and Light Volume voxel shares its rays across the whole graphics card, each bounce checks one light picked by how much it would add instead of testing every light, and the scene is sorted so each ray looks at far less of it. Place Light Probes only checks whether each spot is inside something, so it no longer bakes the lighting of spots it throws away.
  • Direct Samples sets the rays aimed at your lights apart from Samples, because light straight from a lamp needs fewer rays than the bounce. Min Bounces traces every bounce up to it and lets dim paths past it stop early, so deep bounces cost less and the light comes out the same.
  • Knows how much graphics memory you have. When a lightmap at Max Atlas Size would not fit in your graphics card's memory while it bakes, the baker bakes smaller lightmaps at the same detail and tells you it did.
  • Bake Regions and Bake Changes. Bake Regions rebakes the lightmaps, Light Probes, Light Volume voxels and reflection probes inside the boxes you tick, and leaves the rest of the scene as it is. Bake Changes rebakes only what changed since the last bake: a moved light is traced again over its reach, and a moved object over the space around it. An adjustable light that only changed colour, Intensity or Indirect Multiplier, or was switched on or off, needs no tracing at all. Edge Blend fades the new light into the old at the edges.

Notes

  • A scene is lit by this baker or by Unity's lightmapper, never both at once. The two fight over the scene's global illumination data and the editor crashes. The window says so at the top and will not bake while Unity's lightmapper is baking or Auto Generate is on. Press Generate Lighting on a scene baked here and the baker hands it over: it clears its own bake, closes every other open scene, and starts Unity's bake again.
  • An object set to receive light from Light Probes still casts shadows and still bounces its colour into the bake. So does one with its Lightmap Scale at 0. That is exactly how you tell Unity an object should light the room without taking lightmap space of its own, and the report does not call these left out.
  • Lightmap Scale on a renderer does what the field says, so raising it on one wall sharpens the shadows there without costing you resolution everywhere else.
  • It bakes scenes, not avatars, and it knows which project it is in. In a world project everything it bakes uploads with the world. In an avatar project it lights your test scene, so you can see your avatar under baked light, light probes, Light Volumes and reflections. Nothing from it uploads with an avatar there, the window says so, and anything on an avatar stays out of the bake and out of Make Missing Lightmap UVs.
