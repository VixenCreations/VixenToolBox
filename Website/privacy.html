3. This Website

We don't set cookies, we don't run analytics or ads, there are no forms, and nothing on the site stores anything in your browser. The site is static pages and nothing else.

  • Hosting. GitHub Pages hosts the site. When your browser loads a page, GitHub receives your IP address and the usual request details, as any web host does. GitHub handles that under the GitHub General Privacy Statement.
  • Download and version counters. The VPM Registry page loads two small files from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com) to show the download count and the latest version.
  • Discord widget. The VPM Registry page shows our Discord server through a widget loaded from discord.com. Discord receives your IP address and may use its own cookies, under the Discord Privacy Policy.
  • YouTube video. The Get Help Here page embeds a YouTube video. YouTube, owned by Google, receives your IP address and may set cookies, under the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Maths rendering. The Research page loads KaTeX from jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) to draw equations. jsDelivr receives your IP address when it serves the files.
  • Links. Links to Gumroad, Patreon, Ko-fi, Jinxxy, Cash App, X, YouTube, GitHub and other sites take you to services we don't run. Their own privacy policies apply there.

4. Vixens Toolbox

The toolbox runs inside the Unity editor on your computer. It does not connect to the internet, and it sends nothing to us or to anyone else.

  • Settings. A few tools remember your choices in Unity's own editor preferences on your computer, for example the Scene View Enhancer's frame rate cap and the last version the update badge saw.
  • Your project. Tools write files into your Unity project only when you use them, such as PNG copies, Quest copies of an avatar, PhysBone blueprints and presets.
  • Updates. The update badge compares the installed version with the one it saw last, on your computer. Downloads and updates come through the VRChat Creator Companion, which fetches our package listing from GitHub under VRChat's and GitHub's own policies.
  • Links. Buttons in the Hub that point to our website, Discord or store pages open them in your web browser.

5. The Shaders and the VixForge Editor

VixenWear Latex Ultra, VixForge Toon, VixenWear Clothing Pro, VixForge Fur Pro, VixForge World Surface and VixForge World Fur run on your graphics card, in Unity and in VRChat. In VRChat they read lighting and audio data inside the world you're in, such as AudioLink or Light Volumes, and none of it leaves your computer. The VixForge Editor, the material inspector they ship with, sends nothing to us either.

  • Settings. The VixForge Editor and the Shader Hub remember your choices in Unity's editor preferences on your computer.
  • Translation editor. Two optional features in the inspector's translation editor go online, and only when you use them. Auto Translate sends each untranslated line of interface text to Google Translate (translate.googleapis.com). A spreadsheet address, if you type one in, is downloaded from the address you gave. Neither one sends anything about you beyond what any web request carries.

6. VixForge Camera Pro

Camera Pro is an avatar asset. It does not connect to the internet and sends nothing to us. The photos you take are captured and saved by VRChat on your computer, under VRChat's own policies. Buying it on Gumroad gives us only the details described in Supporting us and buying from us.

7. Our Discord Server and Bot

Everything you post in our Discord server is handled by Discord under the Discord Privacy Policy. On top of that, our own bot, VixenGitWatch, runs in the server to keep scammers and raids out, run support tickets, post updates from our GitHub projects and answer a few fun commands. It runs on a computer we operate ourselves. It works only in our server, and it leaves any other server it is added to.

The message log. To review scams, raids and rule breaks after the fact, the bot keeps a log of every message posted by a person in the channels it can see, including staff messages and edits. For each message it records:

  • the channel, your username, your Discord user ID and the message ID;
  • the text, up to 2,000 characters;
  • the name, link, type and size of each attachment;
  • the title, link, image link and the start of the description of each embed;
  • stickers, poll questions and answers, forwarded messages, which message you replied to, how many people and roles you mentioned, and whether it was a voice message.

The log is a set of files on the bot's computer. Once the current file reaches 10 MB a new one starts, and only the three files before it are kept, so the oldest entries are deleted as new ones come in.

Automatic moderation. The bot checks messages for scams as they arrive:

  • It compares images and stickers with a folder of known scam images, using a fingerprint it works out on its own computer. It only downloads images from Discord's own servers, up to 12 MB each, and no outside service sees them.
  • It checks text, links, forwarded messages and polls for known scam sites and phrases, and counts mass mentions.
  • It counts how fast each person posts and spots the same message repeated across channels. These counts live only in memory and are gone when the bot restarts.
  • When we switch it on, a join check looks at how old a new member's Discord account is, and a raid mode acts on everyone who joins during a sudden wave of joins.

When a check is triggered, the bot can delete the message and time out, kick or ban the account. It posts a record of what it did (the account, the action, the channel and the reason) to a private staff channel. Members with moderator permissions are never checked.

Support tickets. When you run /ticket create, the bot makes a private channel named after your username that only you, the server owner, our listed staff and server administrators can see. The channel's topic holds your user ID so the bot knows whose ticket it is. When the ticket is closed, the bot saves a transcript to a text file on its computer (each message's author, user ID, text, attachment links, embed titles and descriptions, and sticker names) and then deletes the channel. Transcripts are kept until we delete them.

GitHub updates. GitHub sends news of activity in our public repositories (new code, releases, issues and pull requests) through a relay service, smee.io, and the bot posts it in a channel. The bot keeps the raw notices in files on its computer, up to 64 MB per file with the two files before it kept. They hold what GitHub sends: the public activity itself and the GitHub usernames of the people involved.

Fun commands. Mentions of the bot and commands such as /joke and /roll get a reply. A short per-person cooldown is kept in memory. Nothing is saved.

The bot's working log. A separate log records errors, the actions the bot took, and the bot leaving servers it was not meant to be in. It keeps one current file of up to 5 MB and the two before it. When we switch on detailed logging to track a problem, it also records each message's author, channel and size (not its text) and each new member's account age.

Supporter roles. If you link Patreon or Ko-fi to Discord, their own integrations give you your supporter role. That link is between you, Discord and that platform.

8. Supporting Us and Buying From Us

Payments go through Gumroad, Jinxxy, Patreon, Ko-fi, PayPal or Cash App. They take your payment details under their own privacy policies, and we never see your full card or bank details.

  • What we receive. What the platform shows creators: usually your name or username, your email address, what you bought or which tier you joined, the amount, the date, and sometimes your country.
  • Free downloads. Getting a free shader on Gumroad still goes through a Gumroad checkout, so we receive the same details for it.
  • Getting your access. Patreon and Ko-fi give you your Discord role automatically. PayPal, Cash App and Gumroad do not, so for those you send us a message with your proof of purchase and the email or username you used, and we give you access by hand.
  • The supporter wall. Some tiers include your name on the supporter wall on our Support page and in the Shader Hub inside the shaders. Ask and we'll leave you off it or take you down.

9. GitHub

Issues, comments and pull requests you post on our GitHub repositories are public, and GitHub handles them under its own privacy statement. Our download counter only adds up GitHub's total download numbers for each release. It never sees who downloaded anything.

10. Email and Direct Messages

If you email us or message us on Discord, we get your address or username and whatever you write. We use it to answer you and keep it as long as we need it for that, or for the records described below.

11. Why We Use It

We use information only for these reasons. If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, the legal basis for each is in brackets.

  • Keeping the Discord server safe: the message log, automatic moderation, join checks and staff records (our legitimate interest in protecting members from scams, spam and raids).
  • Helping you: tickets, transcripts, emails and messages (our legitimate interest in giving support, and performing our agreement with you when you are a supporter).
  • Delivering what you paid for: purchase and membership records and Discord roles (performing our agreement with you).
  • Keeping financial records that tax law requires (legal obligation).
  • Thanking supporters on the supporter wall (our legitimate interest, and you can opt out at any time).
  • Dealing with legal claims or protecting our rights and other people's safety (legitimate interest, or legal obligation).

12. Who Else Sees It

  • The services we use, each for its own part: Discord, GitHub, smee.io, Gumroad, Jinxxy, Patreon, Ko-fi, PayPal, Cash App, and Google when you use Auto Translate.
  • Our server staff see the moderation records in the staff channel and the tickets they are added to.
  • Discord, if we report an account for breaking its rules.
  • The authorities, if the law requires it, or if it is needed to protect someone's safety.
  • Whoever takes over a project, if one is ever handed on. We would tell you first.

That's the whole list. Nobody gets your information to advertise to you.

14. Your Rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us to:

  • tell you what we hold about you and give you a copy;
  • correct anything that is wrong;
  • delete it, including your entries in the bot's logs and your ticket transcripts;
  • stop using it for a purpose, or take you off the supporter wall.

Email Vixenlicious@outlook.com from an address we can match to you, or tell us your Discord username so we can check it's you. We'll answer within 30 days. We may keep something you asked us to delete if we need it to keep a banned account out, to deal with a legal claim, or because the law makes us keep it, and we'll tell you when that happens. Messages you posted on Discord itself are yours to delete there.

In the EEA or the UK, you also have the right to restrict how we use your information, to object to our legitimate-interest uses, to receive your information in a portable form, and to complain to your data protection authority.

In California and other US states with privacy laws, you have the right to know, correct and delete your personal information. We don't sell it or share it for targeted advertising, so there is nothing to opt out of, and we will never treat you differently for using any of these rights.

15. Keeping It Safe

The bot's logs and transcripts stay on a private computer we control. They are never published, and only we can open them. The bot's Discord access key is kept private. No setup is perfectly secure, so if something ever goes wrong in a way that affects you, we'll tell you and, where the law requires it, the authorities.

16. Age Requirement

Our server, products and memberships are for people aged 18 and over. We don't knowingly collect information from anyone under 18. If you think someone under 18 has given us information, email us and we'll delete it.

We're in the United States, and the bot's computer is too. The services we use process information in the United States and other countries. Where information about people in the EEA or the UK goes to the United States, those services rely on their own legal safeguards for the transfer, such as standard contractual clauses.
